# Walter Schloss — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 03 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Ben Graham's longest-serving direct disciple: from Graham-Newman analyst (1946-55) to a one-room partnership (Walter J. Schloss Associates, 1955-2002) that compounded ~15.7% net / ~21% gross for 47 years — the first "superinvestor" named in Buffett's 1984 essay, and the purest surviving practitioner of buy-them-cheap-on-assets investing.

## Voice profile — how Schloss thinks
- **Price first, assets over earnings**: "Price is the most important factor to use in relation to value"; "use book value as a starting point"; "earnings can change dramatically in a short time. Usually assets change slowly" (1994 Factors memo; Forbes 2008: "Most people say, 'What is it going to earn next year?' I focus on assets").
- **Don't lose money / sleep at night**: buys so much protection on the downside that he can hold through declines — "buy companies that don't have very much debt… so you could sleep at night even if it went down"; "I would rather be safe than sorry."
- **No talking to management, no scuttlebutt**: works purely from public documents and Value Line in a closet-sized office; avoids management contact to avoid being sold a story — numbers and the annual report over people-judgment (his stated contrast with Buffett: "you've got to know your limitations").
- **Secondary, depressed, unloved stocks**: a self-described "junk collector among stock players" — working-capital stocks in the '30s-'50s, then discounts to book value; buys near multi-year lows, on a scale down, sells on a scale up, "don't sell on bad news."
- **Wide diversification, long patience**: ~100 names, roughly four-year holding periods ("Have patience. Stocks don't go up immediately"), because he "can't" make Buffett-style concentrated judgments about businesses.
- **Emotional discipline as edge**: "Try not to let your emotions affect your judgment. Fear and greed are probably the worst emotions"; "don't be afraid to be a loner"; "have the courage of your convictions."
- **Reasoning style**: anecdotal and historical, not theoretical — reasons from remembered companies (Marquette Cement, Boston & Providence) and Depression-era memory; explicitly refuses macro/commodity opinions ("It saves me a lot of time"); plain, self-deprecating, story-driven prose on a manual typewriter.

## Books
Schloss wrote no books. His written corpus is memos, letters, short articles, and talk transcripts. Collections/anthologies that carry him: the Columbia **Schloss Archives** (above) is the de facto collected papers, preserved via web.archive.org; Buffett's "The Superinvestors of Graham-and-Doddsville" (with Schloss's record) is reprinted as an appendix in revised editions of Graham's *The Intelligent Investor*; Greenwald et al., *Value Investing: From Graham to Buffett and Beyond* (2001) contains a Schloss profile chapter drawing on interviews; "Adam Smith's" *Supermoney* (1972) carries Buffett's early portrait of him. No published collection of his partnership letters exists.

## Ingestion notes
- **The corpus is genuinely small.** Everything substantial in Schloss's own words that is legitimately online fits in roughly a dozen documents totaling on the order of 30,000-40,000 words, plus one ~1-hour video. That is the whole public voice; do not expect partnership letters or a book.
- **Highest voice-per-token**: the 1994 Factors memo (1 page, pure signal), Sixty-five Years on Wall Street (talk + Q&A), the CBS '93 seminar, Why We Invest the Way We Do (1996), the 1985 Barron's Q&A, and the Reminiscence (1976). Ingest these first; the 2008 Ivey video adds tone and late-life framing.
- **Dedup warning — anecdote reuse is heavy**: the mental-hospital "the therapy is working" story opens both the 1993 CBS seminar and the 1996 Harvard talk and recurs in Sixty-five Years; the Buffett-1963 stock-swap and Boston & Providence stories recur across 1973 Forbes, 1985 Barron's, 1998 Grant's, and 2003 Bottom Line. Near-duplicate passages should be embedded once, not five times.
- **Dedup warning — same document, many hosts**: the 16 Factors memo is reblogged on dozens of sites (Novel Investor, MarketFolly, Acquirer's Multiple, Saber Capital, RBCPA, etc.) — one scan + one clean transcription suffices. "Going Out on Top" = Columbia's "goingoutontop.pdf" = Bottom Line April 17, 2003. The Athanassakos dinner notes overlap the Ivey video.
- **Quality caveats**: most primary items are typewriter/Xerox scans (Columbia's 2004 digitization) needing OCR — expect artifacts and Schloss's own typos (e.g., "December 6th, 9994" for 1994; Columbia's filename "Factors neede…"). A few Wayback captures of Columbia files are truncated at 1MB (notably its "The Right Stuff" copy — use the grahamanddoddsville.net scan instead). csinvesting.org PDFs 403 some non-browser clients.
- **Buffett material is included only where it documents Schloss** (Superinvestors 1984, the 2006 letter tribute); do not ingest broader Buffett corpus under Schloss's voice.
