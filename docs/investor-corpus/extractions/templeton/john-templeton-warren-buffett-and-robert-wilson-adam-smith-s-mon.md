## Other

- **The prayer-centered decision process, stated as his true point of differentiation**: Asked directly how he differs from Philip Fisher and Warren Buffett, Templeton names worldwide scope and longer earnings-power horizons first, but reserves his most personal and distinctive answer for last — that every directors meeting and every decision opens with prayer, framed explicitly as a discipline for reducing (not eliminating) mistakes.
  > "And certainly because I rely on prayer in everything we do. We open all our directors meeting with prayer. We pray about every decision we make. In the long run, that means we'll still make hundreds of mistakes but less mistakes than otherwise. We try to follow Solomon and pray for wisdom." @line 508

- **Geographic exile as a deliberate discipline, not an accident**: Templeton frames living 1,000 miles from Wall Street not as a lifestyle preference incidental to investing but as an active technique for preserving contrarian independence — directly crediting the move to the Bahamas with 22 years of fund performance that beat the prior 25 years run from Radio City, New York.
  > "We did move here (to the Bahamas) because it is so very pleasant. We now have found that for the 22 years we lived here, the performance of our mutual fund is better than the 25 years when we managed from Radio City in New York." @line 490

- **The despondent-selling motto as a founding creed**: Adam Smith elicits the actual published motto of Templeton's investment counsel business — coined near the firm's founding, decades before this 1985 interview — framing "buy when others are despondently selling, sell when others are avidly buying" not as a slogan invented for the interview but as the firm's institutional doctrine since day one.
  > "we published the motto of our business: to buy when others are despondently selling and to sell when others are avidly buying requires the greatest fortitude and pays the greatest rewards." @line 494

- **The interviewer's closing synthesis of shared character traits**: Adam Smith's concluding words explicitly link Templeton, Buffett, and Wilson not by method but by temperament — independence of mind, willingness to act alone against the crowd, and trust in their own perceptions — a framing that matches Templeton's own emphasis elsewhere on character and psychological discipline as prerequisites for investment success.
  > "They have Independence of minds. They are not afraid to act alone when the crowd is going the other way. They trust their own perceptions." @line 555
