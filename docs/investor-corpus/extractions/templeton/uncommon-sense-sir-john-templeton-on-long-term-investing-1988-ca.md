## Other

- **A sermon on gratitude delivered months after Black Monday**: The framing detail that Templeton gave this speech to The Canadian Club just months after the October 1987 crash — and chose to open with a "lengthy sermon on gratitude and optimism" rather than tactical market commentary — captures his signature move: reframing crisis conditions as evidence for optimism rather than a reason for retreat.
  > "coming just a few months after the devastating Black Monday crash, he surprised the crowd with a lengthy sermon on gratitude and optimism." @line 18

- ""Centuries of human enterprise...bursting forth into flower"": This evocative phrase — used to describe the modern era of progress the speech's first half is built around — exemplifies Templeton's rhetorical habit of casting economic and scientific advancement in almost devotional language, treating material progress as itself a kind of spiritual flowering.
  > "centuries of human enterprise are now miraculously bursting forth into flower" @line 20

- **The ten-blessings-per-problem ratio as investing psychology**: Rather than a market-timing rule, Templeton offers a ratio for mental discipline itself — for every problem, ten blessings — presented as the antidote to the negativity bias he believed caused investors to miss opportunity during downturns; it is his psychological counterpart to "buy at maximum pessimism."
  > "Because for every problem you or the nation has, there are ten blessings." @line 32
