## Other

- **A 1961 Cold War letter from an investment counselor**: This document is remarkable for what it is not — a market or stock-picking letter — sent instead to clients as "one of a series of talks on general world conditions," revealing that Templeton saw geopolitical ideology (freedom versus dictatorship) as directly relevant material for the people entrusting him with their capital.
  > "January 9, 1961, one of a series of talks on general world conditions." @line 26-28

- **"Not redistribute the wealth but multiply the wealth"**: In his closing statement of what "the true idealist" believes, Templeton crystallizes his entire economic philosophy into a single aphoristic contrast — production and growth over redistribution, freedom over security, opportunity over regulation — a formulation that reads as the ideological seed of his later global, growth-seeking investment style.
  > "The true idealist preaches not class hatred but universal love; not redistribute the wealth but multiply the wealth; not more regulation but more freedom; not security but opportunity." @line 163-166

- **Spiritual purpose folded into an economic argument**: Mid-letter, without transition, Templeton pivots from a discussion of dictatorship and freedom into a claim about the purpose of human life itself — that discovery and challenge serve spiritual growth — showing how inseparable his economic and religious convictions were even in a document nominally about "free competition."
  > "Possibly the purpose of life on earth is to help human minds and souls, by discovery and challenge, toward spiritual growth and understanding of the natural universe." @line 50-53

- **Framing the Cold War as "World War III" fought in living rooms**: Rather than treating the Cold War as a distant military or diplomatic contest, Templeton insists it is already underway and its battleground is intimate and domestic — "living rooms, work rooms, classrooms" — a framing that turns everyday persuasion and belief into the frontline of the struggle for free markets themselves.
  > "The battleground is the unsuspecting, disarmed and defenseless living rooms, work rooms, classrooms , board rooms, parish halls, union halls and club rooms of America and other free nations." @line 148-151
