## Question patterns

- **pattern**: Where are the cheapest stocks available across all markets and industries? — askWhen: during valuation phase, anchor: business-model
  > "Where are the cheapest stocks? Which stocks are the lowest price? Which industry? Which nation? Or what type of stocks?" @line 131

- **pattern**: Is this area's unpopularity permanent or temporary in nature? — askWhen: evaluating contrarian positions, anchor: business-model
  > "We search for nations or industries where the stock prices are extremely low and they are only extremely low when there are good reasons for it." @line 135

- **pattern**: How will this company or sector perform under inflation and increased government intervention? — askWhen: risk-analysis phase, anchor: business-model
  > "So more than ever before we are searching for shares of companies that will not suffer much from inflation or maybe even would benefit from inflation." @line 143

- **pattern**: Has our strategy been so successful for so long that self-satisfaction is now the bigger risk than being wrong? — askWhen: evaluating whether to continue a strategy that has recently outperformed, anchor: culture
  > "There's always a temptation, when you've had unusually favourable performance, to be self-satisfied and to think that you have found the answer so you will just continue with it." @line 129

- **pattern**: Would twelve independent analysts agree with this pick — and if so, is that itself a warning sign? — askWhen: sanity-checking a consensus recommendation, anchor: business-model
  > "If you get the same answer from twelve security analysts about which stock to buy you can be pretty sure it's the wrong stock." @line 112

- **pattern**: Is the country or region that houses this business tending toward socialism/nationalization in ways that threaten the specific asset (banks, mines, oil, railways, rents)? — askWhen: assessing political-regime risk before committing capital, anchor: business-model
  > "in our investment work one of our major problems is to find out which stocks are not likely to be greatly hurt by socialism." @line 143

## Search directives

- **directive**: Search global markets systematically for stocks priced lowest relative to estimated value using standard security analysis — queryShapes: price-to-value ratio, global coverage, sourcePriority: fundamentals over sentiment
  > "We search anywhere in the world to see which stock is available at the lowest possible price in relation to its estimated value." @line 123

- **directive**: Identify industries or nations experiencing temporary adverse sentiment where underlying fundamentals justify optimism — queryShapes: contrarian positions, macro headwinds, sourcePriority: history-of-reversals
  > "We search for nations or industries where the stock prices are extremely low and they are only extremely low when there are good reasons for it." @line 135

- **directive**: Prioritize unpopular holdings where smart capital (companies themselves, sophisticated acquirers) signals mispricing through buybacks or takeovers — queryShapes: insider action, M&A activity, sourcePriority: revealed-preference
  > "Companies are buying in their own shares more than they ever did in the past because the people who run them know that their stock is selling for a fraction of what it's worth." @line 159

- **directive**: Cross-check a national regime's history of nationalizing banks, mines, oil companies, railways, or regulating rents before committing capital in that jurisdiction — queryShapes: "has this government nationalized this industry before?", "are rent/price controls in force?", sourcePriority: comparative country studies of socialism/regulation history
  > "we study all nations where information is available to see what happened where socialism has become serious. Did they nationalize the banks or the mines or the oil companies or the railways? Did they regulate rents so that they became uneconomic?" @line 143

- **directive**: Track pension-fund allocation percentages into common stocks over time as a forward liquidity/demand indicator for equities — queryShapes: "what fraction of new pension money is flowing into stocks this year vs. peak years?", sourcePriority: pension fund flow statistics
  > "Last year they put in only nine per cent of their new money into common stocks. But the time will come when again they will be putting fifty per cent into common stocks." @line 163

- **directive**: Compare current index price to replacement-cost asset value, not merely historical book value, when judging how cheap a market is — queryShapes: "how does the current index level compare to the replacement cost of underlying assets?", sourcePriority: asset replacement-cost studies, index book-value data
  > "consider the replacement cost of those assets. I have spoken only of their original cost, but the replacement value is far higher than their book value. Never has there been a time when common stocks were so cheap in relation to their replacement value." @line 159

## Other

- **Four scripture-derived quotations as the true capstone of an investing speech**: Templeton closes an address on "Successful Investing Methods" not with a valuation forecast but with four spiritually-rooted quotations he says carry "more wisdom than all the security analysts I have ever met" — the clearest single piece of evidence in this document that he regarded character and eternal perspective as senior to technique.
  > "These four quotations have more wisdom than all the security analysts I have ever met." @line 175

- **The Daon case study of debt reframed as opportunity**: Templeton describes buying a heavily indebted (90%) Canadian real-estate company, explicitly reversing his own younger-self instinct to avoid such leverage — because in an inflationary regime the small equity slice captures all the appreciation — and reports it returned ten times his cost, an unusually concrete illustration of flexible, non-dogmatic thinking overriding an inherited rule.
  > "we bought shares of a Canadian company called Daon about five or six years ago because it had ninety per cent debt. Forty years ago we would have stayed away from it because any company that's got ninety per cent debt in its structure is risky. But we took the viewpoint that in the real estate field where Daon is, if ninety per cent of their capital was in the form of debt then the owners of the ten per cent would receive the total appreciation. That has been one of our fortunate selections–worth ten times what we paid for it five years ago." @line 143

- **The Rothschild "philanthropists" aphorism as a lesson in liquidity-provision**: Templeton relays an old Rothschild-family line — "We always buy cheap and sell dear. We are philanthropists" — using ironic humor to teach that a contrarian investor is, functionally, providing liquidity to panicked sellers and euphoric buyers alike, a service framing rarely stated so plainly elsewhere in his corpus.
  > "We always buy cheap and sell dear. We are philanthropists. Sometimes people are extremely anxious to sell things and are willing to try to find a buyer at any price and so we buy and accommodate them." @line 139

- **Founding the Templeton Prize framed by his introducer, not himself**: The chairman's introduction — delivered before Templeton speaks — notes his founding of the Templeton Foundation Program of Prizes for Progress in Religion and his role as President of the Board of Trustees of Princeton Theological Seminary, establishing that his spiritual and financial identities were publicly fused well before his later-life foundation work became widely known.
  > "He is also very much a man of other parts. He is the Founder of the Templeton Foundation Program of Prizes for Progress in Religion." @line 99
