## Concepts

- **title**: Separation of stock price from real underlying value — cash surplus overwhelming stock scarcity creates mispricings — question/test: Can markets misprice based on mechanical supply/demand rather than fundamental earnings?

## Other

- **The "more buyers than sellers" parable**: Templeton relays a Wall Street anecdote — a client asking why stock prices went up and being told simply "because there were more buyers than sellers" — using humor to make the point that price action can be a mechanical supply/demand phenomenon divorced from underlying value, a caution against over-interpreting rallies as fundamentally driven.
  > "He replied Simply, "Because there were more buyers than sellers"." @p.2

- **Searching for tomorrow's quality before the market agrees**: Written in 1954, this letter already shows the core of what would become Templeton's signature: don't chase stocks the market has already crowned "top-quality" — go looking for the company that will earn that reputation years from now, while it is still cheap and unknown.
  > "A greater ultimate reward may be achieved by searching now for those" @p.2-3

- **Middle-ground discipline against market timing**: Rather than urging clients to go all-in or all-out based on his own market view, Templeton counsels holding close to a personal "normal" proportion in stocks — an early articulation of humility about forecasting and a preference for disciplined consistency over conviction-driven timing.
  > "We believe that each investor should have now close to his "normal" Proportion in common stocks - certainly no more and in most cases no less - with the rest held in safer investments as a reserve." @p.3
