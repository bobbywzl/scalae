## Other

- **The Yale origin story of global investing**: Templeton traces his signature worldwide-diversification approach to a specific moment at Yale, watching wealthy classmates invest only in America and judging that narrowness "egotistical" — a rare biographical anchor for why he became the pioneer of international value investing decades before it was fashionable.
  > "The other boys at Yale came from wealthy families, and none of them were investing outside the United States, and I thought, 'That is very egotistical. Why be so shortsighted or near-sighted as to focus only on America? Shouldn't you be more open-minded?" @line 186

- **Fusing spiritual humility and investment risk management into one lesson**: In the single quote most directly bridging his two vocations, Templeton claims that four and a half decades as an investment counselor taught him humility, and that humility in turn taught him the necessity of worldwide diversification — making risk management not a technical rule but a downstream consequence of his character.
  > "In my 45-year career as an investment counselor, humility did show me the need for worldwide diversification to reduce risk." @line 176

- **The Templeton Prize and the science-religion reconciliation project**: The Wikiquote biographical framing identifies his signature philanthropic act — founding the Templeton Prize for Progress Toward Research or Discoveries about Spiritual Realities — showing that his post-investing career sought to apply scientific rigor to spiritual questions rather than treat the two domains as separate.
  > "Sir John Marks Templeton ( 29 November 1912 – 8 July 2008 ) was an American-born British stock investor, businessman, philanthropist and founder of the John Templeton Foundation and the Templeton Prize for Progress Toward Research or Discoveries about Spiritual Realities ." @line 144

- **"Maybe you're both right" as a signature conciliatory stance**: Rather than picking a side in the science-versus-religion debate that consumed much of his post-retirement life, Templeton's own words show a distinctive move — reframing apparent opposites as partial truths, mirroring the same open-mindedness he applied to markets he didn't yet understand.
  > "When he hears scientists quarrel with believers, he thinks both sides are missing the broader point. "What I'm trying to do is say: 'Don't try to argue — maybe you're both right'..."" @line 202
