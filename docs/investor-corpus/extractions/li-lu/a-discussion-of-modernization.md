## Concepts

- **title**: Civilization 3.0 compounding loop (S&T × free market) — question: Is this business's growth powered by the self-reinforcing loop of accumulating technology/knowledge plus free-market competition that Li Lu calls "Civilization 3.0" — a loop with no structural ceiling — or is it still bounded by an old-economy hard ceiling on a resource, license, or protected channel?, test: Identify the specific mechanism by which each new unit of technology/knowledge lowers the company's costs or expands its addressable market, and confirm that mechanism is still actively running (not a one-time historical step-change already fully captured)., evidence: Li Lu's account of how modern S&T plus free markets produced open-ended compounding growth unprecedented in agrarian history
  > "A pattern of compounding economic growth over a long span of time began to develop that seemed to have no ends." @line 2207

- **title**: The sixth cardinal relationship — honesty toward strangers as a trust multiplier — question: Does the company extend to strangers (public shareholders, anonymous customers, arms-length counterparties) the same honesty that Chinese relational culture traditionally reserves only for the five cardinal relationships (ruler-subject, father-son, husband-wife, elder-junior, friends)?, test: Look specifically at conduct toward parties with no personal or reciprocal tie to management, since Li Lu argues this is precisely the relationship traditional culture leaves unregulated and where a multiplier effect — of trust built, or of loss from its absence — shows up fastest., evidence: Li Lu's proposal that honesty-toward-strangers become a "6th cardinal relationship" woven into the traditional five, with an explicit multiplier effect on both reward and penalty
  > "Hence, Chinese culture must propose a 6th cardinal relationship to define and govern relationships between strangers." @line 2698

- **title**: Creative destruction vs. the bureaucratic leviathan — question: Could a government administrator plausibly have authorized both this company's rise and its rival's fall — or only an unplanned competitive market could have produced this outcome?, test: Name the incumbent that would have had to be "allowed to fail" for the current leader to win; if no planner could plausibly have sanctioned both the failure and the win, weight the outcome as durable market-driven creative destruction rather than policy-directed allocation a future policy shift could reverse., evidence: Li Lu's Eastman Kodak / Xerox / AT&T-and-Apple examples of creative destruction no government would have chosen
  > "It would be hard to imagine that a government would have chosen to completely destroy AT&T while making Apple, a computer company on the verge of bankruptcy, the winner with the largest market capitalization in the world." @line 2460

## Metrics

- **name**: Investment-to-GDP bottleneck threshold (Li Lu) — formula as stated: gross investment approaching ~50% of GDP, confirmed by visible overbuilt/idle capacity ("ghost towns") rather than the ratio alone, reading: once investment nears 50% of GDP and investment waste such as ghost towns appears, investment-driven growth hits a bottleneck and durable growth must shift to domestic consumption instead, benchNotes: use official fixed-asset-investment/GDP ratios as the proxy, but treat a high ratio as a warning only when corroborated by a named, observable instance of idle or overbuilt capacity — Li Lu pairs the ratio with physical evidence, never the ratio alone, anchor: business-model
  > "once investment approaches 50% of GDP and investment wastes such as "ghost towns" began to appear across the country, the investment-driven GDP growth will also encounter a bottleneck" @line 2400

- **name**: Value-creation multiplier — 1+1>2 (goods/labor) vs. 1+1>4 (ideas) — formula as stated: Ricardian division-of-labor exchange of goods/services ≈ 1+1>2; free-market exchange of ideas under modern S&T ≈ 1+1>4, because each party keeps its own idea, gains the other's, and new ideas emerge from the exchange itself, reading: a qualitative multiplier, not a literal computation — Li Lu's shorthand for why knowledge/idea-exchanging businesses (open R&D, platforms, networks) compound value faster than businesses that only trade discrete goods, benchNotes: not a computable financial ratio for the cleansing bench — use only as a qualitative screen for whether a business's economics resemble combinatorial knowledge exchange (higher multiplier) or discrete goods trade (bounded, Ricardian gains only); never substitute for reported figures, anchor: business-model
  > "He used the formula 1+1>2 to illustrate this concept." @line 2178

- **name**: Morris Social Development Index (four-trait composite) — formula as stated: four traits — energy capture per person, social organization (population of the largest settlement), information technology, war-making capacity — each capped at 250 points, summing to 1000 at the year-2000 Western benchmark; earlier or other societies are scored by ratio against that benchmark, reading: a historical composite-comparability index; Li Lu's broader point is that a civilization's — or by extension an economy's — development level should be benchmarked on demonstrated capacity to capture and deploy resources across multiple dimensions, not one flow metric alone, benchNotes: illustrative/historical only, not a bench formula to import into company analysis — cite only for the discipline of building weighted, multi-dimensional comparability indices across very different entities or eras, anchor: business-model
  > "Morris divides the ability of a society to capture and expend energy into four traits: energy capture per person, social organization, information technology, and war-making capacity." @line 224
