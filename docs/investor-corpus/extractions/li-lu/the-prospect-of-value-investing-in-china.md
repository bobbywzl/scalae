## Concepts

- **title**: Stock as Fractional Ownership — question: Are we underwriting the business's own compounding, or trading paper detached from it? test: A share is ownership before it is a tradable instrument; wealth should track intrinsic value compounding with the economy, not the daily tape.
  > "Stock is not only a tradable security but also a certificate representing fractional ownership in the company indeed." @line 457-458

- **title**: Mr. Market as Servant, Not Teacher — question: Is a price move being read as new information about value, or is the market mistaken for the authority on value? test: The market exists to transact with, not instruct — it quotes a price for a stake at any moment but never certifies what that stake is worth.
  > "For value investors, the market exists to serve them and offers an opportunity to acquire ownership." @line 468-469

- **title**: Real Risk Is Permanent Capital Loss, Not Volatility — question: Is "risk" here a real threat to capital, or price movement inside a business still understood and still cheap to intrinsic value? test: Risk turns real once a position sits outside the circle of competence; inside it, swings are noise a margin of safety absorbs.
  > "Only in this sense (investing outside of circle of competence) is there real risk in the market, which is not ups and downs in stock prices, but rather permanent loss of capital." @line 512-513

- **title**: Civilization 3.0 — Free Market Plus Technology as the Growth Engine — question: Is the jurisdiction this business depends on still inside the free-market-plus-technology system that drives sustained compound GDP growth, or has it exited that system? test: Sustained, multi-decade compound GDP growth is a modern (~300-year-old) phenomenon unique to economies combining free exchange with technology; a market's compounding case stands or falls with its place in that system.
  > "I believe China is at interim stage between Civilization 2.0 and Civilization 3.0." @line 1008
