## Concepts

- **title**: Wealth as share of purchasing power, not an absolute number — question: is this compounding my real share of the economy, or a static number that erodes as the economy grows? test: once growth compounds, static assets (cash, land) become relative liabilities; only businesses growing faster than the economy raise one's share. evidence: British nobles' land/castles now cost more to maintain than they yield; China's "ten-thousand-yuan households" are no longer wealthy after 340x nominal GDP growth.
  > "Thus, the fundamental purpose of investing is to preserve and increase your purchasing power." @line 626

- **title**: Macro is accepted; the investor's edge lives entirely at the micro/company level — question: am I trying to trade the macro backdrop, or channeling it into company-specific work? test: the macro "exists objectively" and can't be traded around; returns come from judging businesses inside whatever regime is given. evidence: Graham built the method in the Depression/WWII; Li Lu's own strongest vintages fell inside the 1997-98 Asian crisis and China's bear market.
  > "Firstly, our fundamental attitude as a value investor is that the macro environment exists objectively; we can only accept it, while the micro level is where we can make a diﬀerence." @line 561

- **title**: Circle-of-competence boundary is confirmed by drawdowns, not comfort — question: has this holding actually been price-tested, and did I hold it because I understood the business or got lucky? test: an untested circle of competence is unverified; understanding is confirmed only when a severe decline forces re-examination and the thesis still holds. evidence: BYD held 22 years through six 50%+ drawdowns (one 80%+) while sales grew from roughly RMB 1B toward RMB 1T.
  > "Only then is your circle of competence truly tested; touching its boundaries conﬁrms its existence." @line 947

## Metrics

- **name**: No-leverage drawdown-withstand threshold (Himalaya Capital policy) — formula as stated: no-borrowing rule sized so the fund can withstand a 50% peak-to-trough portfolio decline without forced selling. reading: leverage, not business quality, turns an ordinary drawdown into a forced sale. bench notes: a risk-policy threshold, not a filings ratio — reads redemption/forced-selling risk, and how much of a target's drawdown is leverage-forced elsewhere in the market.
  > "because only by doing so can we withstand a drop of 50% in the entire investment portfolio." @line 958

- **name**: P/E ratio, conditional on earnings-quality decomposition — formula as stated: uninformative until earnings are classed cyclical/peak-margin vs. genuinely stable. reading: a low P/E can mean cheap or mean earnings are at a cyclical peak about to mean-revert. bench notes: decompose the earnings line before trusting any multiple built on it — complements, not replaces, the bench's normalized-earnings work.
  > "are the earnings cyclical? If the P/E is low, is it because it is at the peak of the cycle, because the earnings include many one-time or cyclical elements, or because the earnings are genuinely long-term, stable, and sustainable?" @line 1033

- **name**: Hidden-asset book value composition (TCI case) — formula as stated: $300M market value vs. $500M book value, $400M of it one traded security (TCI) carrying ~$100M of wireless-license value not yet analyzed. reading: a cheap price/book screen can conceal a second, undiscovered layer of underpriced assets. bench notes: flag unexplained balance-sheet line items rather than treat a book-value screen as fully priced — an anecdote, not a repeatable ratio.
  > "My ﬁrst "ten-bagger" had a market value of $300 million and a book value of $500 million, $400 million of which were shares in a publicly listed company, TCI, which later became the largest cable TV company in the U.S." @line 1021
