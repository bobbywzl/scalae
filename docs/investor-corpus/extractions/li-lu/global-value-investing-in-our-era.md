## Concepts

- **title**: Wealth as share of purchasing power, not an absolute number — question: is this compounding my real share of the economy, or a static number that erodes as the economy grows? test: once growth compounds, static assets (cash, land) become relative liabilities; only businesses growing faster than the economy raise one's share. evidence: British nobles' land/castles now cost more to maintain than they yield; China's "ten-thousand-yuan households" are no longer wealthy after 340x nominal GDP growth.
  > "Thus, the fundamental purpose of investing is to preserve and increase your purchasing power." @line 626

- **title**: Macro is accepted; the investor's edge lives entirely at the micro/company level — question: am I trying to trade the macro backdrop, or channeling it into company-specific work? test: the macro "exists objectively" and can't be traded around; returns come from judging businesses inside whatever regime is given. evidence: Graham built the method in the Depression/WWII; Li Lu's own strongest vintages fell inside the 1997-98 Asian crisis and China's bear market.
  > "Firstly, our fundamental attitude as a value investor is that the macro environment exists objectively; we can only accept it, while the micro level is where we can make a diﬀerence." @line 561

- **title**: Circle-of-competence boundary is confirmed by drawdowns, not comfort — question: has this holding actually been price-tested, and did I hold it because I understood the business or got lucky? test: an untested circle of competence is unverified; understanding is confirmed only when a severe decline forces re-examination and the thesis still holds. evidence: BYD held 22 years through six 50%+ drawdowns (one 80%+) while sales grew from roughly RMB 1B toward RMB 1T.
  > "Only then is your circle of competence truly tested; touching its boundaries conﬁrms its existence." @line 947

- **title**: Venice → Netherlands → Britain as a genealogy of the modern capital-market credibility system — question: does this jurisdiction's financial infrastructure trace to a genuine multi-century credibility-building lineage (double-entry bookkeeping, joint-stock companies, dispute resolution, rule of law), or is it a shallow, recently-assembled facsimile? test: check whether the jurisdiction's institutions (courts, exchanges, intermediaries) have survived a real stress event without being overridden by fiat. evidence: Venice invented double-entry bookkeeping and insurance; the Netherlands added the public limited-liability company and a central bank/stock exchange; England's 1688 "merger" transplanted the entire Dutch financial system and thereafter never lost a European war nor defaulted on debt issued several times its GDP.
  > "This merger brought a complete modern financial system to Britain." @line 337

- **title**: The virtual-vs-real economy distinction as an obsolete 2.0-era category — question: is a "real economy" vs. "virtual economy" policy distinction still doing useful analytical work, or is it importing an early-industrialization-era category into a science-tech economy where it no longer maps onto anything real? test: name a company (NVIDIA, a drone-software firm) that the distinction cannot cleanly classify; if such cases proliferate, the category itself, not the company, is the problem.
  > "This blurs the line between virtual and real economies, making the distinction largely irrelevant." @line 240-241

- **title**: The "chicken-and-egg" interconnected-nodes model of a stalled economy — question: rather than searching for the single root cause of an economic slowdown, which node in the interconnected chain (entrepreneur confidence, consumer confidence, bureaucratic incentives, foreign capital trust, bilateral relations, capital-market health) can actually be ignited first? test: any node, once lit, should cascade to the others if the chain truly is interconnected — track whether an intervention at one node produces movement elsewhere.
  > "every node is both "chicken" and "egg," and all can "lay eggs."" @line 492
