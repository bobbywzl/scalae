## Other

- **The founding origin story: fleeing a stereotype of the stock market via Cao Yu's "Sunrise", then meeting Buffett by accident**: Li Lu describes arriving in America with an image of stock markets formed by Cao Yu's 1930s Shanghai play "Sunrise" — "full of cunning deceits, luck, and bloodshed" — and only attended Buffett's lecture because a classmate mentioned a speaker whose name sounded like "buffet." This is Li Lu's most extended personal account of how a culturally-inherited negative image of markets was overturned by direct observation of Buffett's character, not by economic argument alone.
  > "Prior to this, my impression of the stock market was that of Shanghai in the 1930s as depicted by Cao Yu's play "Sunrise" - full of cunning deceits, luck, and bloodshed." @line 57-58

- **The seven-years-to-friendship arc: how Li Lu and Munger's relationship actually formed**: Li Lu explicitly narrates the multi-year, non-linear formation of the relationship — an unremarkable first meeting where Munger seemed "distant and absent-minded" and "probably had no impression of me then," followed by years of occasional contact, before a decisive 2003 Thanksgiving conversation seven years later in which Munger diagnosed Li Lu's investment problems as "practically all the problems of Wall Street" and offered to invest with him if he abandoned the conventional hedge-fund model. This is Li Lu's own granular account of the specific turning point that reorganized his entire firm and career.
  > "At a Thanksgiving gathering in 2003, seven years after we first met, we had an in-depth, sincere discussion." @line 189-190

- **The repeated-early-arrival vignette as evidence of character, escalating across four data points**: Li Lu builds a deliberately structured four-meeting anecdote — arriving on time, then 15 minutes early, then 30 minutes early, then a full hour early at 6:30 AM — always finding Munger already seated reading newspapers, until Li Lu concludes Munger simply always arrives early and uses the extra time productively rather than wasting it. This escalating structure is a rare piece of Li Lu's own narrative craftsmanship used to make an abstract virtue (discipline, non-waste of time) empirically undeniable rather than merely asserted.
  > "At the fourth meeting, I arrived an hour early and sat there waiting at 6:30 AM. At 6:45, Charlie strolled in with a pile of newspapers and sat down, not even looking up, completely unaware of my existence." @line 362-364

- **The airport security anecdote as an unplanned test of character under inconvenience**: Li Lu recounts an unscripted, non-staged incident — Munger's repeated failures to clear an airport security detector causing him to miss his flight entirely — and observes that Munger's response was to calmly read a book rather than show frustration, explicitly offering this as evidence of temperament under petty adversity rather than the polished self-presentation of a scheduled interview.
  > "Charlie did not seem upset. He took out a book he carried with him and sat down to read while he waited for the next flight." @line 382-383

- **Reframing Munger as a reincarnation of the Confucian Literati, not merely an American businessman**: In Part 3, Li Lu makes his most distinctive intellectual move in the piece — explicitly arguing that after twenty years in America he has come to see Munger not through a Western lens but as the modern embodiment of the traditional Chinese scholar-official (Literati) archetype, someone whose commercial success is inseparable from moral self-cultivation, and speculates that "if Confucius were to be reborn in America today, Charlie would likely be the ideal incarnation." This is the clearest statement in Li Lu's corpus of how he fuses value investing with a specifically Chinese cultural and philosophical framework.
  > "Over the years that I've known Charlie, I have often forgotten that he is an American. In my mind, he is more similar to the traditional Literati (scholar-officials) of Imperial China." @line 450-451
