## Concepts

- **title**: Intellectual honesty as foundation for investment edge — defined as distinct competency buckets
  > "Investing is about intellectual honesty. You want to know what you know. You want to know, mostly, what you don't know. If understanding the management team is not in the cards, it's not in the cards." @p.29

- **title**: Circle of competence as dynamic and expandable through business study — shaped by personal interest rather than predetermined boundaries
  > "I let my own personal interests define my circle of competence. Obviously I know something about China, Asia, and America – those are things that I am really familiar with." @p.25

- **title**: Value investing as binary inoculation not gradual adoption — either the principle clicks immediately or not at all
  > "There are few people that switch in between or get it gradually. They either get it right away or they don't get it at all." @p.24

- **title**: Investing as zero-sum competitive game requiring informational and analytical superiority
  > "You really want to be sure that you are better informed and better reasoned than the person on the other side of the trade." @p.25

## Metrics

- **name**: Himalaya Capital compensation structure — formula adopted from original Buffett partnership model
  - No management fee charged to investors
  - 6% annual return provided to investors at no cost (hurdle rate)
  - 25% of returns above 6% taken as general partner carry
  - 100% of general partner capital invested in fund (no separate account management)
  > "We don't take any management fee. We provide a 6% return for free to our investors and then take 25% after that. I don't invest anything outside of the fund. I put all of my investment capital into my funds. So it's a true partnership." @p.29

- **name**: Three occasions for sell decisions — framework for portfolio adjustment discipline
  1. When analysis is proven wrong (even "correct mistakes" where low-probability event occurs)
  2. When valuation swings to extreme euphoria or pessimism
  3. When discovering materially better opportunity to improve portfolio
  > "One should make sell decisions on one of three occasions. Number one, if you make a mistake, sell as fast as you can, even if it's a correct mistake." @p.31

- **name**: Management quality assessment as material but difficult component of investment decision
  > "Management always has a big influence on your success, no matter how good or how bad the business is itself. Management is always part of the equation of making the company successful, so the quality of management always matters. But to assess that quality is not that easy." @p.28
