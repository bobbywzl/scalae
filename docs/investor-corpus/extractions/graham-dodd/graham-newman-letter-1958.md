## Other

- **The dissolution letter itself**: This is the final letter to Graham-Newman Corporation stockholders — the corporation had already been dissolved by the time of writing, with the former directors now acting as trustees. The two-sentence body ("The Corporation has been dissolved...") stands in stark contrast to the detailed multi-page strategy letters of 1946-1955, marking the formal end of the vehicle through which Graham ran his partnership investing career.
  > "The Corporation has been dissolved. The Directors are acting as Trustees for the benefit of stockholders and we are pleased to enclose « copy of the report of the auditors for the year ending January 31,1958, which shows an oversll profit for the twelve months." @line 10-14

- **All five principals signing as Trustees, not officers**: The closing signature block lists Jerome A. Newman, Benjamin Graham, Robert J. Marony, David L. Dodd, and Howard A. Newman collectively as "Trustees" rather than by their prior corporate titles (President, Chairman, Treasurer, etc.) — a small but telling documentary marker of the corporation's legal transformation from an operating investment company into a winding-up trust.
  > "Jerome A, Newmen Benjamin Graham Robert J. Marony David L. Dodd Howard A. Newman Trustees" @line 21-28

- **The New Haven Railroad litigation as Graham's own unresolved legal exposure**: The letter's opening explicitly conditions final accounting to stockholders on resolution of "the New Haven matter" — a lawsuit arising from a 1955 put-option arbitrage trade in New York, New Haven & Hartford Railroad securities, in which Graham-Newman itself was named a defendant alongside the railroad's own directors. It shows Graham, near the end of his active investing career, personally exposed to litigation risk from what the firm's own accountants describe as a still-undetermined potential loss.
  > "An action has been commenced against certain directors of New York, New Haven & Hartford Railroad Company and a large group of other defend-~- ants who had transactions with that company, ~ in which suit Graham-Newman Corporation has been named as one of the defendants." @line 487-490
