## Metrics

- **name**: Earnings-to-price ratio relative to bond yields — formula: Earnings-to-price ratio ≥ 2 × (AAA corporate bond yield); equivalently P-E ≤ 100 ÷ (2 × bond yield); minimum threshold always allows P-E of 7, maximum never exceeds P-E of 10, reading: In 1976 with AAA bonds yielding 7%, this yields maximum P-E of 7; Graham treats this as the binding mechanism to ensure stocks offer appropriate margin of safety relative to current risk-free rates
  > "I select only those issues whose earnings-to-price ratio-simply the P-E in reverse-is at least twice the average current yield on top-quality (triple-A) corporate bonds" @line 118

- **name**: Financial leverage test via equity-to-assets ratio — formula: Stockholders equity ÷ Total assets ≥ 50%; equivalently total assets ÷ total liabilities ≥ 2, reading: Company must own at least twice what it owes; Graham frames this as a simple, readily-available test requiring only data from annual reports or broker inquiries
  > "A company should own at least twice what It owes. ,An e~sy way to check on that is to look at the ratio of stockholders, eqUlty:o total assets; if the ratio is at least 50 percent, the company s financIal condition can be considered sound" @line 150

- **name**: Holding period discipline with fixed exit threshold — formula: Set 50% profit target for each position; exit all positions within 2-3 years regardless of profit status, reading: Graham's 50-year research showed that applying this dual-exit rule (first-to-occur of 50% gain or 2-year threshold) across a diversified 30-stock portfolio yields approximately 15% annual returns plus dividends
  > "First you set a profit objective for yourself. An objective of 50 percent of cost should give good results" @line 179

- **name**: Portfolio size for statistical reliability — formula: Minimum portfolio of 30 stocks across holdings; can use odd lots if capital constrained, reading: Graham emphasizes that statistical probability operates reliably only with sufficient diversification; he recommends 30-stock minimum
  > "To give yourself the best odds statistically, the more stocks you have to play with, the better. A portfolio of 30 would probably ~; an ideal minimum" @line 174

- **name**: Bond-Yield-Reactive Stock/Bond Capital Allocation Band — formula: allocate up to 75% of investment capital to common stocks when many names pass the screen (e.g., during a broad market decline like 1974); drop to ≤25% in stocks (rest in government bonds) when the market as a whole is overpriced and few names pass, reading: the allocation band is driven purely by the count of currently-eligible stocks, not by macro forecasting, benchNotes: this is the portfolio-level counterpart to the single-stock P-E/equity screen — the screen's output count feeds directly into the stock/bond split
  > "you should take advantage of the situations and put up to 75 percent of your investment capital into common stocks" @line 197-198
