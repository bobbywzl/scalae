## Other

- **1945 dating and institutional context**: This is Graham's affirmative side of a formal point-counterpoint debate published in The Analysts Journal (January 1945), responding to a 1942 proposal from the Committee on Standards of the New York Society of Security Analysts for a "Qualified Security Analyst" (Q.S.A.) designation — a rare document showing Graham arguing institution-building and professional credentialing rather than valuation technique.
  > "In 1942, the C o m m i t t e e on Standards of the N e w Y o r k Society ot Security Analysts proposed to the m e m b e r s h i p that a rating or protessional title be established tor security analysts. This rating was designated tentatively as "Qualified S e c u r i t y Analysd" or "Q. S. A."" @line 8

- **The C.P.A. analogy as generational memory**: Graham opens by reaching back "some fifty years" to when accountants themselves were debating the then-controversial C.P.A. designation, using his own vantage point in 1945 to argue that what feels radical today becomes "taken for granted" within a career-length span — an argument built on Graham's own sense of professional history.
  > "S o m e fifty years ago, trained accountants were wrestling with a similar idea, and at that time the difficulties and drawbacks of the proposed C. P. A. designation no doubt appeared quite serious to m a n y of them." @line 32-36
