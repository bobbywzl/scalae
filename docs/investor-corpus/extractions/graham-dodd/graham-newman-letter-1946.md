## Other
- **Graham-Newman's tenth-anniversary framing**: The letter opens by explicitly marking its own institutional milestone — the fund's tenth year of operations concluding January 1946 — and uses that anniversary as the stated reason to depart from custom and include extended commentary rather than a terse annual report, a small but real piece of the partnership's own self-narrated history.
  > "on January :31st, 1946, the-Company concluded its tenth year.of operations." @line 5
- **Signed jointly by Graham and Jerome Newman**: The letter closes with both partners' signatures and titles — Graham as President, Jerome A. Newman as Vice-President & Treasurer — a direct documentary record of the Graham-Newman Corporation's leadership structure in the mid-1940s.
  > "Benj. Grahan, President Jerome A. Newman, - | Vice-President & Treasurer" @lines 165-169
- **A candid acknowledgment of underperformance risk in continued bull markets**: Rather than promise continued outperformance, Graham states plainly that the fund's more conservative policy means it is "not likely" to match common-stock gains "as long as the present rising market continues" — an unusually direct admission, in an investor letter meant to reassure clients, that discipline sometimes means giving up upside.
  > "As long as the present rising market continues it is not likely that we shall be able to match the percentage gain shown by common stocks generally." @line 108-110
