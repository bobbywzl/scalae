## Concepts

- **title**: "Cheap AND good, not cheap OR good" (the Magic Formula thesis) — combining a crude quality screen (high return on tangible capital) with a crude value screen (earnings yield) and ranking by both, tested by decile from 1987 forward; the joint long-only ranking beat lower deciles consistently, but a long/short version of the same signal could still be wiped out in a single bad year (2000), so the edge must be held long and patient, not levered against its own volatility
  > "it turned out that the top decile beat the second decile, beat the third decile, beat the fourth decile." @line 284

- **title**: "Size positions by loss-risk, not by gain-potential" — position size should track how little you stand to lose if the thesis is wrong, not how much you could make if it is right; this is what let a well-understood special situation grow to an outsized share of the portfolio without feeling reckless
  > "I didn’t size my positions based on how much money I could make. I took bigger positions, you’re able to take bigger positions in things you can’t lose much money in." @line 95

- **title**: "The screw loose" (temperament as the scarce edge) — durable success in concentrated, contrarian investing requires a temperamental tolerance for pain that lets the investor recover psychologically within days rather than being paralyzed; framed as near-prerequisite, not a nice-to-have
  > "you have to have a little bit of a screw loose to take the pain, especially with a very concentrated portfolio that a number of people I know, pursue." @line 173

- **title**: "Owning businesses vs. owning quotes" — the identical concentrated allocation reads as prudent when framed as buying six or eight private businesses on a multi-year horizon, and as reckless when re-priced daily as volatile stock quotes; the underlying risk hasn't changed, only the marking and framing have, which is exactly what unmarked private-equity vehicles exploit to "smooth the ride"
  > "No one would say you’re crazy because you’re thinking of them as businesses. You divided your investment in its businesses." @line 140

## Metrics

- **name**: Magic Formula quality screen — return on tangible capital — formula as stated: "high returns on tangible capital," a deliberately crude proxy for business quality drawn from reading Buffett's letters; reading: higher = better business, used to rank stocks into deciles jointly with the value screen; bench notes: explicitly not a refined multi-factor score — chosen to be simple enough to compute across the whole market
  > "So crude metric for good was high returns on tangible capital if you read through Buffet’s letters, that’s really what he’s looking for." @line 281

- **name**: Magic Formula value screen — earnings yield vs. safe-rate benchmark — formula as stated: compare the yield from owning the business's earnings (e.g. "10% yield") against a safe alternative like a bank deposit (e.g. "5% in a bank"); reading: the wider the spread over the safe rate — especially with expected growth — the more attractive the bet; bench notes: this is the "cheap" half of the Magic Formula, combined by decile with the quality screen above, not blended by a stated numeric weight
  > "if you could get 5% in a bank and you could get 10% yield by owning the earnings of this business, and you thought it was going to grow, that sounds better." @line 281

- **name**: Forward two-year return estimate, S&P 500 vs. Gotham's deep-value "Value 1000" portfolio (as stated in this March 2022 interview) — formula as stated: apply the same 30-year historical valuation-based model to the S&P 500's then-current pricing and to the ~600-700 stock cheap-weighted Value 1000 portfolio; reading: model implied mid-single-digit returns for the S&P 500 over the next two years vs. roughly 35%-40% for the value portfolio over the same period; bench notes: explicitly a backward-fit historical model, not a guarantee ("Doesn't mean that'll happen")
  > "it says mid-single digit returns over the next two years. Whereas that value portfolio using the same analysis says, I think it’s going to earn 35% to 40% over the next couple of years, based on past history." @line 323
