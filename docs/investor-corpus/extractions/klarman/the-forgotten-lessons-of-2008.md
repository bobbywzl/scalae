## Concepts

- **title**: False sense of security — How normalized excesses in lending and capital markets mask true systemic risk until crisis hits. Test: Identify when bad practices become standard industry behavior; evidence: lax lending persists for years before triggering collapse.
  > "When excesses such as lax lending standards become widespread and persist for some time, people are lulled into a false sense of security, creating an even more dangerous situation." @line 52

- **title**: Price versus intrinsic value illusion — The latest trade creates dangerous mispricing during exuberance; market price is not reliable guide to value. Test: Compare recent trading prices to private market valuations and fundamental business economics.
  > "The latest trade of a security creates a dangerous illusion that its market price approximates its true value. This mirage is especially dangerous during periods of market exuberance." @line 62

- **title**: Leverage propagation risk — Uncontrolled leverage by other market participants creates systemic portfolio risk even for unleveraged investors through forced selling and correlation breakdown.
  > "Even if you are unleveraged, the leverage employed by others can drive dramatic price and valuation swings; sudden unavailability of leverage in the economy may trigger an economic downturn." @line 76

- **title**: Behavioral governance of markets — Markets follow behavioral science patterns of overreaction and momentum, not mathematical models; risk is psychological and momentum-driven, not computable.
  > "Despite the predilection of some analysts to model the financial markets using sophisticated mathematics, the markets are governed by behavioral science, not physical science." @line 58

- **title**: LBO equity as disguised out-of-the-money option — question: at what purchase multiple does an LBO's equity tranche stop being ownership and become a leveraged bet on a narrow recovery path?; test: check whether the deal's leverage level and price paid leave equity value dependent on a specific favorable outcome, the hallmark of an option rather than a business stake
  > "Many LBOs are man-made disasters. When the price paid is excessive, the equity portion of an LBO is really an out-of-the-money call option. Many fiduciaries placed large amounts of the capital under their stewardship into such options in 2006 and 2007." @line 78

- **title**: Liquidity premium discipline — question: is the yield or discount offered for illiquidity actually adequate, especially when that illiquidity comes without control rights?; test: compare compensation received for locked-up or non-controlling positions against the opportunity cost of being unable to exit or influence outcomes during stress
  > "Be sure that you are well compensated for illiquidity – especially illiquidity without control – because it can create particularly high opportunity costs." @line 72
