## Concepts

- **title**: Absolute return over relative return — question: are we compounding capital in real, inflation-beating terms, or merely tracking a benchmark index regardless of whether that index is itself cheap or dangerously concentrated?; test: size and hold positions to clear an absolute inflation-beating hurdle; treat "behind the market" as irrelevant once the hurdle is cleared
  > "We want to beat inflation by hundreds of basis points." @line 116

- **title**: Downside protection buys the right to play offense — question: if this environment gets sharply worse, does the position's or fund's structure force us out (margin call, redemption, covenant breach) before the thesis has time to play out?; test: structure sizing and liquidity so survival through the worst case is never in doubt, because the payoff of protection is the ability to buy when overexposed competitors cannot
  > "if you protect on the downside, if you don't find yourself getting margin calls, frozen in place because you're too exposed, or getting massive redemptions because you're down so much" @line 72

- **title**: Bottom-up mispricing over top-down positioning — question: did this idea start from a specific, checkable security mispricing, or from a macro/sector view we then went shopping to express?; test: require the thesis to originate in a concrete bottom-up observation, and use the top-down "weather" only to size risk, never to generate positions
  > "if you're going to climb a mountain, you want to look bottom-up: is this the right trail, is it safe, do I have my equipment, am I prepared?" @line 108

- **title**: Holding inconsistent ideas simultaneously — question: can the analyst hold "this is true today" and "this is likely to become less true or untrue at some unknown point" about the same macro condition without needing to resolve the tension into a single forecast?; test: check whether a thesis is built to survive a regime change rather than betting on the current regime persisting indefinitely
  > "you know that what seems to be true today for all time probably won't be true for all time, and you hold on to that. So again, it goes to the idea of holding inconsistent ideas in your head at the same time" @line 140

- **title**: Cash as concentrated-portfolio byproduct, not a top-down macro call — question: does the fund's cash level arise mechanically from position sizing (large positions coming off) rather than from a forecast about the market's direction?; test: check whether cash swings track realized exits from 5-10% positions rather than a stated view on valuations broadly
  > "With concentrated positions, we have 5% and 10% positions in the portfolio. When two or three of them come off, cash goes from next to nothing to 15% or 20%. So that's the origin, that's how we got started with the idea that we would hold some cash from time to time." @line 112

- **title**: Versatile-athlete team structure over narrow specialization — question: can the same analyst move between private investments, credit, and equity within one opportunity, or is the team siloed by asset class such that busy periods in one area strand talent in another?; test: check whether personnel cross-train across a firm's opportunity groups rather than being permanently assigned to a single asset class

## Metrics

- **name**: Absolute-return inflation hurdle (Klarman) — formula: stated as "beat inflation by hundreds of basis points" — a portfolio-level required-return hurdle, not a per-security ratio; reading: this is the bar the whole book must clear before relative-to-market performance is even worth discussing, freeing the portfolio from needing to track a concentrated, expensive index; benchNotes: a discount-rate/required-return input for a DCF or a normalized-earnings-yield comparison, never itself derived from EBITDA — anchor: business-model

- **name**: Distressed entry price vs. covered-at-par recovery — formula: purchase price in cents on the dollar (e.g., "buy this bond at 70") compared against an independently underwritten recovery ("covered at par"); reading: the margin of safety in a distressed credit is the gap between what fear prices in and what the capital structure/collateral actually supports — buy when that gap is wide; benchNotes: requires the analyst's own asset-coverage/recovery waterfall built from cleansed balance-sheet lines, never a sell-side price target or an EBITDA-multiple recovery assumption — anchor: business-model
  > "We're saying, oh, I can buy this bond at 70 that I think is covered at par." @line 104
