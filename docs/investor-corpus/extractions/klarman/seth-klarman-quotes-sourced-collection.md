## Concepts

- **title**: Margin of Safety — a protective cushion built into investment analysis that accounts for uncertainty and estimation error
  > "Margin of safety is simply the idea that you want room to be wrong." @line 153

- **title**: Risk Relativity — risk is not an inherent property of a security but emerges from the price paid relative to intrinsic value
  > "Risk is not inherent in an investment; it is always relative to the price paid." @line 545

- **title**: Capital Preservation — the foundational duty of investors before pursuing growth or returns
  > "The first and foremost responsibility of every investor is preservation of capital." @line 857

- **title**: Bankruptcy emergence as catalyst — question: does this cheap security have a mechanism that forces value realization, or could it stay cheap indefinitely?; test: check for a process event (bankruptcy reorganization, spin-off, litigation resolution) that converts undervaluation into distributed value on a knowable timeline
  > "A cheap stock can stay cheap forever, but if you own a bankrupt bond, the process of emerging from bankruptcy and distributing new securities offers a practical catalyst to realize the value." @line 297

- **title**: Concentration over diversification — question: does spreading capital across many small positions actually reduce risk, or does it just dilute the investor's ability to know each holding well?; test: check whether the portfolio is built from a limited number of closely monitored, high-conviction positions rather than broad, shallow diversification
  > "Rather than own a little bit of everything, we have always tended to place our eggs in a few dozen baskets and watch them closely." @line 665

## Metrics

- **name**: Intrinsic Value Discount Target — buying securities at substantial discounts to calculated intrinsic value; Klarman's formulation targets 50-cent dollars
  > "We try to buy dollars for 50 cents, and to realize the dollar before too much time passes." @line 345

- **name**: Downside/Upside Risk Asymmetry — comparative measurement of the ratio of possible losses to possible gains at current price
  > "The best investors do not target return; they focus first on risk, and only then decide whether the projected return justifies taking each particular risk." @line 521

- **name**: Perpetual-ownership valuation anchor — formula: ask "what would we be willing to pay to own a security forever?" as the reference price, then require a discount from that figure before buying; reading: forces valuation off a permanent-holding assumption rather than a resale-timing bet, which filters out story stocks whose worth depends only on the next buyer; benchNotes: an intrinsic-value anchoring method, not a per-security financial ratio — pairs with cash-flow-based valuation, never a multiple-of-EBITDA shortcut
  > "The question we ask ourselves is, ”What would we be willing to pay to own a security forever?” Then we determine whether we can buy it at a discount from that figure." @line 337
