## Concepts

- **title**: Margin of Safety as Psychological Foundation — The principle that guides conviction and distinguishes investing from speculation; attained through stress-testing assumptions against adverse scenarios and building conviction that the analysis can tolerate stress
  > "Our approach has always been to find compelling bargains. We are never fully invested if there is nothing great to do. We test all our assumptions with sensitivity analysis. Through stress testing, we gain a high degree of conviction that we are right. We are prepared for things to go slightly wrong because we adhere to a margin-of-safety principle that gives us the necessary courage to go against the tide." @line 275-282

- **title**: Securities as Business Claims, Not Ticker Symbols — Reframing that defuses price volatility by anchoring valuation to cash-flow and intrinsic worth; enables conviction to hold or add during drawdowns
  > "You may be skeptical of my answer, but, yes, it was easy. It is critical for an investor to understand that securities aren't what most people think they are. They aren't pieces of paper that trade, blips on a screen up and down, ticker tapes that you follow on CNBC. Investing is buying a fractional interest in a business and buying debt claims on a business." @line 252-260

- **title**: Investment vs. Speculation — Demarcation rule: investment has contractual cash flow or the likelihood of near-term cash flow independent of exit price; speculation depends entirely on what a future buyer will pay
  > "The line I draw in the sand is that if an asset has cash flow or the likelihood of cash flow in the near term and is not purely dependent on what a future buyer might pay, then it's an investment. If an asset's value is totally dependent on the amount a future buyer might pay, then its purchase is speculation." @line 549-554

- **title**: Client Base as Prerequisite to Investment Excellence — Great clients are more important than investment skill; they free the manager from redemption pressure and short-term performance anxiety, enabling conviction during crises
  > "We have great clients. Having great clients is the real key to investment success. It is probably more important than any other factor in enabling a manager to take a long-term time frame when the world is putting so much pressure on short-term results." @line 169-173
