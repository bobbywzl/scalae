## Concepts

- **title**: Catalyst-driven value investing — selecting securities with explicit catalysts for value realization reduces portfolio dependence on market direction
  > "Positions with catalysts tend to lag a rapidly rising stock market (like this past year's) and outperform a lackluster or declining one (like we used to have every few years!)." @chars 1606

- **title**: Value discipline as navigational tool in market chaos — when fundamental valuation discipline is abandoned, investors lack bearings
  > "In a stormy market, the value investing discipline becomes crucial, because it helps you find your bearings when reassuring landmarks are no longer visible." @chars 4581

- **title**: Story stocks versus money-making stocks — question: is the position being held because it discounts a compelling narrative, or because its mispricing produces an attractive risk/return imbalance?; test: check whether the thesis rests on undervaluation math or on a "great story"
  > "The point of investing, after all, is not to have a great story to tell; the point of investing is to make money with limited risk. At some point, investors will drop their Pulitzer prize winning story stocks and revisit their attention on the old classics, stocks that make you money because their undervaluation creates a compelling imbalance between risk and return." @chars 11072

- **title**: Partial lesson-learning after a bubble — question: has a bursting bubble's correct lessons (price matters, high risk ≠ high return) actually propagated across the whole market, or only to the sector that just blew up?; test: check whether disciplines newly applied to the popped sector are absent elsewhere in the market
