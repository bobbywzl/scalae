## Other

- **the "Read the book" aphorism**: Graham's own advice to Schloss on why Security Analysis was worth reading — the plainest possible statement of how thoroughly Schloss believed the book covered the discipline, and the line that set Schloss's own career in motion.
  > "Read the book and when you know everything in it, you won't have to read anything else." @line 41-42

- **modesty and ethics as the defining trait, not intellect**: Schloss opens his reminiscence by naming Graham's character — high ethical standards, modesty, unassuming manner — ahead of his technical brilliance, an ordering choice that reveals what Schloss himself valued most in a mentor.
  > "Graham was an original thinker as well as a clear thinker. He had high ethical standards and was modest and unassuming." @line 8-9

- **a life-changing act of generosity, plainly stated**: Schloss traces his entire career to one specific, almost incidental act — Graham hiring him as he was leaving the Army in 1945 — described in spare, understated language typical of Schloss's own plain-spoken style.
  > "By offering me a job as his security analyst as I was about to leave the Army at the end of 1945, he changed my life." @line 53-54

- **Dodd's co-authorship as an act of Graham's own generosity**: Schloss preserves the origin story of Security Analysis itself — Graham insisted his notetaker Dave Dodd receive co-author credit even though "Ben did the work," a small anecdote that doubles as a model of the modest generosity Schloss admired.
  > "Ben, using the notes, then went ahead and wrote Security Analysis. As Dave said, Ben did the work but he insisted that Dave get credit by being co-author." @line 61-63
