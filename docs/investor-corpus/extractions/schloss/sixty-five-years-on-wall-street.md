- **title**: Asset-based valuation framework anchoring to liquidation value vs. earnings projection — question/test: Why reject growth forecasting for depressed secondary equities? — evidence in Lens: Schloss explicitly avoided projection-based buys (Xerox case) and defended asset-play positions

- **title**: Contrarian accumulation strategy of buying during price declines — question/test: How does re-averaging into weakness manage downside risk? — evidence in Lens: buying-on-decline discipline documented as core portfolio construction methodology

- **title**: Broad diversification as a defense against ignorance rather than a bet-sizing failure — question/test: Why hold many names instead of Buffett's concentrated "handful of thick bets"? — evidence in Lens: Schloss cites Buffett's own framing and states plainly he cannot invest Buffett's way

- **title**: Delegated turnaround thesis: management/directors, not the investor, are the catalyst for a depressed company's recovery — question/test: What forces a cheap, unloved stock's value to be realized without activist involvement? — evidence in Lens: Graham's/Schloss's reliance on board self-interest as the mechanism of value realization

- **title**: Refusal to hold an opinion on macro/commodity direction as a time- and error-saving discipline — question/test: Does the investor need a view on where copper or silver is headed to buy a commodity-linked stock cheaply? — evidence in Lens: Schloss buys Asarco on statistical cheapness alone, explicitly disclaiming any commodity-price forecast

## Other

- **The mental-hospital heckler story**: Schloss opens his own remarks with a self-deprecating anecdote about being told to "shut up" while lecturing psychiatric patients on investing — and being told that was the most intelligent thing said in months. A hallmark of his plain, unpretentious public persona.
  > "he said, "Shut up, you idiot and sit down!" I looked at my friend and I said, "What do I do?"" @line 38-39

- **Personal history — path into the business**: Capsule biography (delivered by host Jim Grant) of Schloss's unglamorous start as a Wall Street runner in 1934, wartime Army service, and study under Graham before founding his own partnership in 1955 — the "65 years" of the talk's title.
  > "He studied finance under the legendary Benjamin Graham and, following wartime service in the U.S. Army Signal Corps, joined his mentor's firm, Graham-Newman." @line 4-5

- **The Buffett/Xerox near-miss anecdote**: Schloss recounts pitching Graham on what became Xerox (via the Haloid Company/Battelle photocopying process) and being flatly refused because it required projecting a speculative future rather than valuing hard assets — an origin story for his own anti-forecasting discipline.
  > "no Walter. It's not our kind of stock." And, of course, it was Xerox." @line 134-135

- **Buffett's own account of nearly losing control of Berkshire over an eighth of a point**: A story Schloss says Buffett told him personally — Buffett agreed to sell stock back to Berkshire's president at $11.50/share, the tender came in at $11.375, Buffett got angry and bought control instead. Symbolic origin story for Buffett's Berkshire stake, told in Schloss's own words.
  > "The company had previously repurchased stock. The president agreed to buy it from Warren at $11.50 a share." @line 112-114

- **The Warren Buffett stock-buying favor (Genesee & Wyoming Railroad, 1963)**: Personal anecdote of Buffett selling Schloss a small group of stocks Buffett was carrying at cost as a personal favor — one holding (Merchants National Properties) was still in Schloss's portfolio 35 years later at a large multiple of cost.
  > "I bought this from Warren Buffett. He owed me a favor and he had a group of stocks" @line 458-459
