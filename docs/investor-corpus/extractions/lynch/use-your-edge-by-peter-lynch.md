## Concepts

- **title**: Personal Investor Edge — question/test: What asymmetric knowledge domain (profession, hobby, consumer observation, family role) creates investable insight vs sell-side analysis? Evidence: Gerber (parents), Home Depot (DIY adopters), Walmart (shoppers), Dunkin' (uniformed professionals), Cisco (campus network observers). Test: Can I articulate why this company wins using knowledge no Street analyst possesses?
  > "What is it that you know a lot about? Maybe your edge comes from your profession or a hobby. Maybe it comes just from being a parent." @line 357

- **title**: Concentrated Portfolio via Selectivity — question/test: Does 5-10 holding concentration with high-conviction buys outperform fund-manager diversification? Evidence: "one golden egg" theory—single 10/20/50-bagger offsets portfolio drag; you don't need perfection. Test: How many stocks within my edge do I deeply understand; does portfolio size match edge scope?
  > "If you're lucky enough to have one golden egg in your portfolio, it may not matter if you have a couple of rotten ones in there with it." @line 285

- **title**: Growth Duration Bias & Runway Skepticism — question/test: Do investors systematically underestimate how long great operators can sustain growth and geographic expansion? Evidence: McDonald's (1970s "saturated," then 50-bagger); Consolidated Products/Steak & Shake (40 years of growth, still 76% of outlets in 2 states). Test: What geographic/product expansion runway remains vs "nowhere to grow" consensus narrative?
  > "The best companies often thrive even as their competitors struggle to survive." @line 791

- **title**: Combat Theory of Investing — question/test: In competitive platform wars (IBM vs clones, internet browsers, telecom standards), who profits regardless of end-market outcome? Evidence: Microsoft MS-DOS, Cisco wiring, Intel chips—vendors to combatants. Test: Can company prosper across multiple customer/market outcomes, or is fate tied to one winner?
  > "This is the old combat theory of investing: When there's a war going on, don't buy the companies that are doing the fighting; buy the companies that sell the bullets." @line 477

- **title**: Growth Multiple Calibration — question/test: What valuation premium is justified for sustained growth? Evidence: Walmart P/E ≥20 while growing 25-30% annually; multiple never "cheap" but compounding crushed market. Test: Does sustainable growth rate ≥ entry P/E ratio as minimum hurdle?
  > "A key point to remember is that a p/e of 20 is not too much to pay for a company that's growing at 25 percent." @line 661

- **title**: Entry Timing via Proof Point — question/test: When does competitive advantage become "proven" enough to reduce binary risk? Evidence: Microsoft IPO @0.78, 1988 MS-DOS dominance, 1992 Windows 3.1 proof, 1995 Windows 95 scale; each traction point offered entry. Test: Has moat manifested across ≥1 major product/market cycle?
  > "Enter early but not too early. I often think of investing in growth companies in terms of baseball. Try to join the game in the third inning, because a company has proved itself by then." @line 1225

## Metrics

- **name**: Dividend Yield vs Bond Yield Threshold (Equity Exit Rule) — formula: If (LT-Treasury Yield − S&P 500 Dividend Yield) ≥ 6%, exit equities to bonds. Historical reading: 1982 applied at 13-14% treasuries vs 10-11% stock return; 1997 reading ~1.2% gap below trigger (S&P ~2%, treasuries ~6.8%). Bench notes: Indicates opportunity cost inflection; used to time 1982 bond rotation when double-digit inflation made fixed income attractive.
  > "When yields on long-term government bonds exceed the dividend yield on the S&P 500 by 6 percent or more, sell stocks and buy bonds." @line 1105

- **name**: Portfolio Concentration Ratio (Quality Calibration) — formula: 5-10 holdings, each deeply understood; rule of thumb per research intensity: 10 companies screened = 1 viable opportunity; 50 screened = 5 opportunities. Bench notes: Adequate for amateur monitoring; forces disciplined selectivity vs dilution; "one golden egg" principle—need only modest number of compounders.
  > "Investigate ten companies and you're likely to find one with bright prospects that aren't reflected in the price. Investigate 50 and you're likely to find 5." @line 1287

- **name**: Time Horizon to 10-50x Returns (Compounding Window) — formula: Bulk of multi-bagger returns occur 3-4+ years post-entry; full runway 10-15 years (third to seventh inning). Bench notes: Patience discipline required; markets reward buy-and-hold vs rotation trading; contradicts active-trading mythology.
  > "There's plenty of time (10 to 15 years in some cases) between the third and the seventh innings, which is where the 10- to 50-baggers are made." @line 1243

- **name**: P/E Multiple Sustainability vs Growth Rate — formula: P/E of 20 appropriate for 25% sustained growth; premium valuation justified if growth persists. Bench notes: Wal-Mart exemplar—never looked cheap (P/E ≥20) yet compounded returns; inverse relationship between apparent valuation "bargain" and actual opportunity.
  > "A key point to remember is that a p/e of 20 is not too much to pay for a company that's growing at 25 percent." @line 661
