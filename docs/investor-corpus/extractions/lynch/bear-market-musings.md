## Other

- **The "stomach, not brain" framing of investment temperament**: Lynch's signature reduction of investing success to a physical, almost anti-intellectual metaphor — repeated verbatim (with only minor wording variation) in his later Fidelity interview, showing it as a durable, recurring personal catchphrase rather than a one-off line.
  > "I mean stomach is the key organ here. It's not the brain." @line 82

- **The casino-bet analogy for horizon mismatch**: a distinctive, vivid gambling image Lynch reaches for to make an abstract risk-management point (matching investment horizon to a known cash need) concrete and memorable — equating an ill-timed one-to-two-year stock bet directly to a roulette wager.
  > "That's just like betting on red or black at the casino." @line 88

- **Base-rate framing of his own era's volatility**: Lynch situates the reader inside a 95-year statistical history (53 declines, 15 bear markets) rather than treating any single decline as exceptional — a distinctly actuarial, unsentimental way of discussing market fear that contrasts with the more anecdotal, franchise-specific storytelling that dominates his Worth columns and "Use Your Edge."
  > "So 15 in 95 years--about once every six years you're going to have a big decline." @line 71-72
