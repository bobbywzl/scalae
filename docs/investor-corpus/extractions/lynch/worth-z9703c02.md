## Concepts

- **title**: Personal Investor's Edge — how proximity, profession, or experience reveals opportunities before the market
  > "What is it that you know a lot about? Maybe your edge comes from your profession or a hobby. Maybe it comes just from being a parent." @line 27

- **title**: Portfolio Concentration and Let-Your-Winners-Run — owning few high-conviction stocks allows one winner to offset multiple losers
  > "If you put together a portfolio of five to ten of these high achievers, there's a decent chance one of them will turn out to be a 10-, a 20-, or even a 50-bagger, where you can make 10, 20, or 50 times your investment." @line 22

- **title**: Growth Rate as Valuation Anchor — P/E multiple is justified by earnings growth rate and duration
  > "Wal-Mart's earnings were growing at 25 to 30 percent a year. A key point to remember is that a p/e of 20 is not too much to pay for a company that's growing at 25 percent." @line 50

- **title**: "Sell the Bullets" Combat Theory of Investing — In a competitive war between rivals (PC clones, browser wars, telecom), buy the supplier that profits regardless of who wins the fight, not the combatants themselves
  > "This is the old combat theory of investing: When there's a war going on, don't buy the companies that are doing the fighting; buy the companies that sell the bullets." @line 36

- **title**: "Nowhere to Grow" Narratives Are Usually Wrong and Must Be Personally Verified — Market consensus that a mature company has exhausted its growth runway is frequently falsified by direct, on-the-ground checking rather than accepted secondhand
  > "These "nowhere to grow" stories come up quite often and should be viewed skeptically. Don't believe them until you check for yourself." @line 54

- **title**: Company Age Is Irrelevant to Remaining Growth Runway — A decades-old regional chain can have as much expansion headroom as a young company if its geographic footprint is still narrow relative to the addressable market
  > "Whether or not a company has growing room may have nothing to do with its age." @line 56

- **name**: Geographic and Market Expansion Headroom — formula: actual market penetration % vs. addressable market %, reading: <20% indicates substantial growth runway

  > "Ask yourself: What will I make if I'm right, and what could I lose if I'm wrong? Look for a risk-reward ratio of three to one or better." @line 80
