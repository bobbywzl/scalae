## Other

- **Explicit "invest in what you know" articulation, twice over**: the lecture's opening thesis statement is one of Lynch's clearest public formulations of the philosophy — both the general rule and the memorable "explain to a 10-year-old" test for whether an investor truly understands a holding.
  > "If you can't explain to a 10-year-old in two minutes or less why you own a stock, you shouldn't own it." @line 151

- **The absurdist jargon-laden "logic chip" product description as a comedic illustration**: Lynch constructs an intentionally incomprehensible technical product description to dramatize why he avoids businesses whose value depends on specifications he cannot personally judge — an extended, theatrical version of his donut/chip contrast used elsewhere.
  > "If you own a piece of crap like that, you will never make money." @line 153

- **Magellan Fund track-record color via the "nine for nine" crash-correlation claim**: Lynch's own quantified account of Magellan's consistency during down markets across his full 13-year tenure, a distinctive piece of track-record color about how closely his fund tracked broad market declines.
  > "When I ran Magellan, in 13 years the market went down nine times. Every time the market went down, Magellan went down. I was nine for nine." @line 229

- **Family-shopping-mall origin story for stock ideas, told through his own daughters**: Lynch names his daughter Mary's Ann Taylor pick and the family's fondness for Body Shop as concrete instances of family-sourced ideas, extending the "invest in what you know" philosophy into an explicitly domestic, personal register.
  > "Our oldest daughter, Mary, likes Ann Taylor. She had to dress up to go to work when she was working a summer in a consulting firm." @line 279

- **Self-deprecating "rent a 12-year-old" folksy advice**: a distinctively playful, folksy recommendation to shadow a child around a shopping mall as informal consumer research, used to answer an audience question from a single, childless investor.
  > "You need to find a 12-year-old and rent him for the weekend and follow this boy or girl around and see where they're shopping." @line 277

- **Golf-vacation-in-Ireland crash narrative, told in first person at a public lecture**: Lynch's own retelling (independently corroborated across his other appearances) of being on a golf trip to Ireland with his wife Carolyn during the October 1987 crash, including the shareholder-reaction golf metaphor ("he's in a trap, this could be a triple bogey").
  > "Carolyn and I planned this great golf vacation to Ireland. We planned to visit one course and stay at a little house and visit another and go all along the west coast of Ireland and play golf." @line 227

- **Biographical framing: took over Magellan at 33, retired at 46**: a concise biographical anchor for his career arc, situating the entire lecture's authority in a well-defined, publicly known tenure.
  > "He took over the fund in 1977 at age 33 and ran it for thirteen years. His success allowed him to retire in 1990 at age 46." @line 141
