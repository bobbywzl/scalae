## Concepts

- **title**: Cycle Recognition as Core Discipline — question/test/evidence in business-model anchor
  > "The flip side of this happy scenario is one in which the local economy is headed south. If leases are turning over, they're going to be renewed at lower rates" @line 131-133

- **title**: Leverage as Double-Edged Tool — test in cyclicity and volatility
  > "Right now, aggressive REITs are using leverage to their advantage. That's fine, but they're going to have to be pretty quick on their feet to avoid being hoisted on that leverage when the cycle moves along." @line 151-154

- **title**: Structural Distribution Requirement Forces External Growth Financing — Because REITs must distribute at least 95% of net income, they cannot fund growth from retained earnings alone and must rely on debt, asset sales, or share issuance — a distinctive capital-structure constraint absent in ordinary corporations
  > "That's not simple, given the requirement that REITs distribute 95 percent of net income. A REIT can't hold its dividend down for a few quarters while it accumulates cash to buy more property." @line 139-141

- **title**: P/E Ratio Is Structurally Unreliable for REIT Comparison — Because different REITs compute "earnings" inconsistently (due to depreciation treatment), the standard newspaper p/e figure cannot be used cross-sectionally, requiring a REIT-specific metric (FFO) instead
  > "The stock tables in the newspaper show a price-to-earnings ratio for every listed REIT. People in the industry tend to ignore it--for good reason. Different REITs figure earnings differently, so p/e ratio isn't much use for REIT-to-REIT comparison." @line 167-170
