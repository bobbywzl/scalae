## Concepts

- **title**: Commodity-dependent businesses go through cycles; peak valuations at peak commodity prices are dangerous traps. — question/test: When earnings per share reaches a record in a cyclical business, is that a sign of strength or peril? Evidence: Sotheby's earned record $1.96/share at $37 stock in 1989 during art bubble, crashed to $10 stock with 25 cents EPS in 1991 as bubble deflated.
  > "It's in the nature of Wall Street to imagine that whenever a company sets a record for earnings, it will go on setting new ones." @line 116

- **title**: Barriers to entry are structural (relationships, expertise, reputation) rather than technological or capital-based. — question/test: Why can't wealthy buyers simply create a competing auction house? Evidence: Wealthy clients demand century-old institutions with deep relationships to galleries, dealers, museums, and expertise built over generations.
  > "Wealthy aristocrats, in particular, are very persnickety about who handles their effects. They want their auction houses to go back at least as far as their antiques." @line 36

- **title**: Duopolies maintain pricing power by coordinating on commissions while competing for clients. — question/test: Does rivalry between two dominant firms reduce consumer benefit? Evidence: Sotheby's and Christie's fought over Givenchy collection yet both raised buyers' commissions from 10% to 15% within months of each other.
  > "Their rivalry is as spirited as the old Macy's versus Gimbels or Harvard versus Yale." @line 68

- **title**: Franchise investing at a discount to peak — a great franchise selling well below its earlier high multiple, while the underlying commodity shows early signs of recovery, is more attractive than the same franchise was at its bubble peak. — question/test: Is the business fundamentally intact (relationships, market share, brand) even though the stock and reported earnings collapsed with the cycle?
  > "this is a more attractive company than it was in 1989, selling for less than half the price while its principal commodity is showing signs of a revival" @line 166

- **title**: Product-line and geographic expansion inside a moat extends the growth runway without diluting the competitive advantage. — question/test: Is the company adding adjacent categories/markets that leverage its existing trust and relationships rather than entering commoditized new competition?
