## Concepts

- **title**: Brand franchise neglect and valuation gap — the cost to shareholder when management diverts capital and attention to adjacent businesses unrelated to core brand promise — test: does stock trade at discount to intrinsic brand value; does investor perception lag execution?
  > "Block has made some mistakes. In particular, it has occasionally been too attracted to the idea of diversifying its business rather than exploiting its name." @line 86-88

- **title**: Core competency constraint as discipline — markets punish competency sprawl when management pursues growth in categories where incumbent advantages do not transfer — evidence: CompuServe loss spiral despite Block's market position
  > "America Online came along and ate CompuServe's lunch. CompuServe lost $186.5 million in 1997 alone." @line 97-98

- **title**: Effective P/E net of cash — the true earnings multiple after subtracting per-share net cash generated by a recent divestiture from the quoted stock price, revealing hidden discount versus the headline multiple — test: does headline P/E overstate the price paid for operating earnings once cash-per-share is stripped out?
  > "Based on 1998 consensus earnings estimates provided by First Call, the price-to-earnings ratio is around 25, about the same as the market as a whole." @line 117-119

- **title**: Structural customer-segment mismatch as disruption shield — a low-cost technological substitute poses little threat when its natural buyer (higher income, complex return) is not the incumbent's core customer (middle income, simpler return) — evidence: TaxCut's own growth funneled back into Block rather than cannibalizing storefronts
  > "Besides, Block owns TaxCut, the second-most-popular tax-return program (it has a market share of about 30 percent)." @line 191-192
