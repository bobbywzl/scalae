## Concepts
- **title**: Company quality trumps industry attractiveness — Management, balance sheet strength, and execution overcome industry headwinds

- **title**: Small-cap alpha and market dominance — Small stocks produce larger absolute gains than large-cap leaders in multi-decade periods
  > "If there was ever a doubt that small stocks make big moves whereas big stocks make smaller moves, the hit parade ought to put it to rest." @line 20

- **title**: Retailer durability and franchisability — Retailers can sustain exponential growth for decades via store replication and geographic expansion
  > "A successful chain of stores can multiply its sales and its earnings at an exponential rate. It happens again and again." @line 129

- **title**: Opportunity in unpopular sectors — Disciplined stock picking in depressed industries (utilities, airlines, commodities) can yield outsized returns
  > "What long-distance company is on the hit parade? Not AT&T; but LDDS Communications." @line 171

- **title**: Retail saturation as the sell signal — a chain's exponential growth phase ends once store density approaches the market's ceiling; the investor's job is spotting that inflection, not just the growth itself — test: Has the company reached "a store in every mall and on every other street corner," or is there still meaningful unsaturated geography to clone into?

- **title**: Understandability as an explicit filter against chasing unowned winners — even in hindsight-obvious hit-parade winners (biotech, high-tech), an investor should skip names whose business he can't explain rather than chase the sector's return — test: Can the investor state in plain terms what the company does and why it earns money, or is the appeal purely "it went up"?
