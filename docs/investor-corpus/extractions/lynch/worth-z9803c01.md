## Concepts

- **title**: Story as investment thesis — the requirement that an investor articulate a coherent, explainable narrative about why a company will succeed
  > "The level of detail here, in these 14 stories, is the minimum any investor should understand before committing money. Do you own stock in five companies? You should be able to write five stories like these." @line 66

- **title**: Earnings visibility — the ability to forecast a company's future earnings with high certainty based on business fundamentals
  > "we like to see those earnings coming. Which company is most certain to meet our estimates? That's the one we want to buy." @line 93

- **title**: Sector selectivity based on earnings stability — cyclical businesses inherently conflict with the goal of predictable earnings growth
  > "It makes sense to be cautious, because if there's an investing minefield in this decade it's the technology sector, in which so many companies catch a cold one week and are hospitalized with pneumonia the next." @line 84

- **title**: Buy-Side Skin in the Game as a Credibility Filter — Recommendations from people who actually deploy their own or clients' capital, pay commissions, and live with the consequences carry more weight than commentary from media/newsletter stock-touters with no position
  > "These seven money managers actually go out and buy stock in the companies they recommend-thousands, even millions, of shares. This isn't an intellectual exercise-they buy shares in a market that doesn't sit still, paying commissions for the privilege and delivering good results despite these penalties." @line 46-50

- **title**: Absence-as-Signal in a Cross-Manager Idea Survey — What a panel of skilled, independent stock pickers collectively avoids (tech stocks, cyclicals) in a hot market is itself informative about where risk/reward has become unfavorable, distinct from any single pick's merits
  > "I suggest you start by thinking about what isn't on it. There are no technology stocks-no Microsoft, no Intel, no Cisco Systems. There are no classic cyclicals-airlines or automakers." @line 74-77
