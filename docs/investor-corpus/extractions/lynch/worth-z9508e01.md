## Concepts
- **title**: The "Diworsification" Reversal — A company abandons diversification strategy; management recognition: corporate discipline, evidence: multi-decade shift toward core operations
  > "If a divisiondoesn't fit in with a company's main line of work, the companysells it or spins it off." @line 31-32

- **title**: Takeover Probability Advantage — Spin-off shareholders face materially higher acquisition probability (14% vs. 3-4% for general companies), evidence: risk-reward asymmetry
  > "the shareholders ina spin-off are three to four times more likely to benefit fromone of these potentially lucrative episodes" @line 58-60

- **title**: Forced Optimization (Low-Hanging Fruit) — Independent management cuts costs the parent company overlooked; evidence: structural change in operational authority
  > "The managersno longer take orders from above and cut costs in obvious waysthat the old regime overlooked" @line 63-65

- **title**: Parent's Emotional and Financial Stake in Spin-off Success — a strong parent company (unlike one forced into a fire sale) has both an ongoing financial interest and a reputational incentive to set the spun-off subsidiary up for success before releasing it
  > "The parent companymay have a financial stake in the success of the spin-off. Certainly,it has an emotional stake. The last thing a parent company wantsis for one of its own projects to flop." @line 66-69
