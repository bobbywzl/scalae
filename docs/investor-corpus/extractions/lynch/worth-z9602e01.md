## Concepts

- **title**: Price vs. Earnings as Market Psychology Indicator — Market price (P) responds to Wall Street expectations and sentiment, while earnings (E) reflect operational reality; the gap between them reveals investor confidence or pessimism about a company's future
  > "E is the reality of a company's performance, whileP is led around by Wall Street expectations." @line 26-27

- **title**: Growth Company Signature Pattern — Consistent year-over-year earnings growth with price oscillating above and below the E line, indicating market cycles of confidence and doubt about the company's sustainability
  > "Once you've identified a growth company from the E line, you follow the P line to get a read on whether the stock is expensive or cheap, relative to its earnings." @line 48-50

- **title**: Wide P-E Gap as Valuation Warning — When price significantly diverges above earnings lines, it signals market overconfidence that may not be justified by fundamentals
  > "The gap between McDonald'sP and its E hasn't been this wide since 1987, just before theOctober crash." @line 85-86

- **title**: Chart Reading Is Not Technical Analysis — Lynch explicitly disclaims market-timing intent; charts serve fundamentals-based bargain-hunting among growth companies, not price-wiggle prediction
  > "I'm not a technical analyst, or a wiggle reader who makes predictions on the direction of the next wiggle. I usecharts to help me find value among growth companies I follow." @line 184-186

- **title**: Cyclical vs. Growth Chart Signature — A cyclical company's E line "wobbles all over" unpredictably, unlike a growth company's steady upward E line, making the same chart-reading method inapplicable to timing cyclicals
  > "Can you see why this chart doesn't fitwith the others? The E wobbles all over. It's the typical courseof a cyclical company, as unpredictable as a hurricane's path." @line 170-172

- **title**: Chronic Underpricing Despite Strong Earnings — A stock whose P persistently trails its E, even amid tenfold earnings-driven appreciation, represents a standing opportunity as long as the earnings growth continues
  > "Yet in spite of its underachieving P, the stock is up tenfold in ten years, thanks to the spectacular rise in theE." @line 163-165

- **title**: Market Recognition Lag for Reliable Growers — A company can compound reliably for years before Wall Street re-rates it as a "reliable fast grower," at which point the price line permanently crosses above and stays above earnings
  > "You can see the pointwhere Wall Street finally recognized Emerson as a reliable fastgrower" @line 123-125
