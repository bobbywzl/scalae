## Concepts

- **title**: "Few good stocks in a lifetime" — the thesis that portfolio success requires concentration on a handful of big winners, not perfection or activity
  > "You only need a few good stocks in your lifetime. I mean how many times do you need a stock to go up ten-fold to make a lot of money? Not a lot." @line 114

- **title**: "Temperament beats timing" — portfolio returns depend more on investor stomach for volatility and holding power than on predicting market turning points
  > "In this business if you're good, you're right six times out of ten. You're never going to be right nine times out of ten." @line 135

- **title**: "Homework vs. speculation" — the gap between informed analysis (understanding business, product, customer behavior) and uninformed gambling
  > "When they buy a refrigerator, they do research. When they buy a microwave oven, they do research. They'll get Consumer Reports . They'll ask a customer "What's your favorite kind of oven?" @line 649

- **title**: "Rock-turning discipline" — investment outperformance correlates directly with effort and curiosity; winner is whoever examines most investment opportunities
  > "The person that turns over the most rocks wins the game. And that's always been my philosophy." @line 206

## Metrics

- **name**: Historical annual return to stocks over long horizons — formula: ~11% total return (capital appreciation + dividends), divided into ~8% corporate profit growth + ~2–3% dividend yield; historical corporate profit growth ~8% annually (doubling every 9 years, quadrupling every 18 years, sixfold every 25 years)
  > "Historically, corporate profits have grown about eight percent a year. Eight percent a year. They double every nine years. They quadruple every 18. They go up six-fold every 25 years." @line 608

- **name**: Market volatility frequency — formula: 53 corrections of 10%+ in 95 years = approximately once every two years; 15 bear markets of 25%+ = approximately once every six years
  > "The market itself is very volatile. We've had 95 years completed this century. In the 95 years so far, we've had 53 declines in the market of 10 percent or more." @line 569

- **name**: Timing impact on returns — benchmark: investing at market high every year for 30 years yields 10.6% return; investing at market low every year yields 11.7% return; difference across 30 years is marginal compared to holding discipline
  > "In the 30 years, 1965 to 1995, if you had invested a thousand dollars, you had incredible good luck, you invested a the low of the year, your return would have been 11.7 compounded." @line 679
