## Concepts

- **title**: "Understandable business over complex tech" — preference for simple, durable consumer products the investor can personally evaluate (a donut, a cup of coffee) over businesses whose value depends on technology he cannot judge
  > "If somebody invents a new logic chip, I'm not going to know about it. No one's every going to invent a better donut." @line 740

- **title**: "Conviction sized against consensus" — willingness to make an unpopular, "crazy"-looking position the fund's single largest holding when personal analysis diverged from prevailing sentiment, rather than sizing to match consensus comfort
  > "This was the largest position in my fund, in Magellan, in 1982, and everybody thought I was crazy." @line 780

- **title**: "Winners mature over years, not weeks" — the payoff on a stock pick typically showed up in the third or fourth year of ownership, not the third week or month, so short-horizon impatience is what forfeits the gain
  > "My best stocks have been the third year I bought them... not the third week, not the third month, the third, fourth year." @line 884

- **title**: "Preparedness, not prediction, survives a crash" — surviving a sudden, historic single-day crash is framed as a function of prior discipline and psychological readiness rather than having predicted the exact drop
  > "I was very well prepared for the crash of 1987." @line 830

- **title**: "Middle-class tastes as research instrument" — the investor's own everyday consumer habits (malls, fast food, retail browsing) functioned as a genuine sourcing mechanism for stock ideas, not just a folksy talking point
  > "One reason Lynch became the stock picker for the middle class was that he had such middle class tastes. He used to prowl shopping malls and watch what people were buying and what stores they were visiting." @line 744-747

## Other

- **"Invest in what you know" in action, pre-slogan**: the segment shows Lynch physically walking a shopping mall, treating ordinary consumer venues as his research floor — the visual, on-camera demonstration of the philosophy that later became his signature catchphrase.
  > "This is like the New York Stock Exchange, right here, you know? Look at over here to Athlete's Foot." @line 749-750

- **Folksy donut-vs-microchip analogy**: Lynch's classic plain-language contrast between a business he can personally judge (a donut) and one he can't (a logic chip), used repeatedly across his public appearances as the emblem of staying within one's circle of competence.
  > "If somebody invents a new logic chip, I'm not going to know about it. No one's every going to invent a better donut." @line 740-742

- **Magellan Fund household-penetration color**: a striking scale statistic on how deeply the Magellan Fund had penetrated ordinary American households by the mid-1990s, evidence of Lynch's outsized cultural reach as a "Pied Piper" for baby-boomer investors.
  > "Today one in 50 American households has money in the Magellan Fund and the customers of Fidelity Investments alone constitute some 4 percent of the country." @line 855-858

- **Biographical framing as "the new Pied Piper"**: the narrator's own biographical introduction of Lynch, crediting him as the figure who turned Magellan from "a little-known mutual fund" into a movement for a whole generation of baby-boomer investors entering the 1982 bull market.
  > "one man more than any other became the new Pied Piper for the millions of baby boomers who were poised to become investors. He managed a little-known mutual fund called Magellan. His name was Peter Lynch." @line 715-718

- **Crash-of-1987 vacation anecdote as market-crash commentary**: a vivid, specific personal narrative of being on vacation in Ireland as the Dow collapsed, illustrating both his emotional distance from day-to-day panic and his eventual, deliberate decision to fly home once the crash accelerated.
  > "We went to Ireland, first trip we'd ever been there. And then on Friday, because of the time difference, we'd almost completed the day and I called and the market was down 115." @line 833-835
