## Concepts

- **Title**: Market Timing as Wealth Destruction — Core insight: The act of trying to predict and avoid corrections through hedging and selling positions destroys far more wealth than corrections themselves; unforced errors compound losses.
  > "Far more money has been lost by investors preparing for correctionsor trying to anticipate corrections than has been lost in correctionsthemselves." @line 70

- **Title**: Growth Stocks as Correction Resilience — Growth companies demonstrate superior correction performance: held ground in 1987 (up 8.5%), provided downside protection in 1990, outperformed massively over long term (230-fold since 1949 vs. 19-fold for cyclicals).
  > "After the infamous 1987 bear market, when investors in the Dowindustrial stocks were traumatized by a 33 percent loss fromthe August top to the October bottom, including the terrifying 508-point one-day drop, investors in growth stocks were still ahead by 8.5 percent and getting a good night's sleep." @line 211

- **Title**: The Dividend Reinvestment Strategy — A portfolio of high-dividend stocks with consistent dividend growth can generate inflation-adjusted income and capital appreciation simultaneously; replacing bonds entirely is viable for long-term investors.
  > "The strategy I'm proposing can offer the best of both worlds: money to live on that normally comes from bonds, and growth that comes from stocks." @line 254

- **Title**: Corporate Profit Growth as Fundamental Value Driver — Stock prices ultimately reflect underlying business profitability; market volatility is noise relative to long-term earnings growth; corrections matter little if holdings remain fundamentally sound.
  > "What makes stocks valuable in the long run isn't "the market."It's the profitability of the shares in the companies you own." @line 367

- **Title**: Bonds as False Ballast — the popular belief that bonds reduce portfolio volatility is challenged; long-term bonds are shown to be nearly as volatile as stocks when rates move, undermining a core justification for holding them.
  > "Unless you're talking about bonds of the short-termvariety (two to four years), the ballast argument is false. Long-termbonds can be almost as volatile as stocks." @line 237-239

- **Title**: Stress-Testing a Strategy Against Worst-Case Sequencing — before recommending the dividend-funded withdrawal plan, Lynch runs it through an immediate 25 percent crash and then a hypothetical 20-year recession, showing the plan still roughly matches or beats a bond's return even under duress.
  > "Fidelity's Bob Beckwitt ran the numbers. In spite of the immediate25 percent loss, if you stuck with the plan, sold shares to augmentthe return, and collected your $146,820 in dividends along theway, at the end of the 20th year your portfolio would be worth$185,350." @line 325-329
