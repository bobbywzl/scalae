# Peter Lynch — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 04 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Manager of Fidelity's Magellan Fund 1977–1990 (~29% annual, $18M to $14B), the most influential popularizer of bottom-up stock-picking; his books, Worth columns, and lectures are core canon for "invest in what you know."

## Voice profile — how Lynch thinks
- **Amateur's edge / buy what you know**: the individual investor who notices L'eggs at the supermarket or a packed Dunkin' Donuts is 6–12 months ahead of Wall Street — "Amateurs can beat the Street because, well, they're amateurs" (Worth, "Use Your Edge"); the L'eggs story retold in the Frontline interview.
- **Know what you own**: his most repeated rule — "If you can't explain to a ten-year-old in two minutes or less why you own a stock, you shouldn't own it" (1994 National Press Club); owning stocks you can't describe is "gambling," not investing.
- **The earnings line**: stock prices follow earnings, full stop; he reasons from 10-and-35-year chart-book overlays of price vs. earnings and P/E-to-growth ("Mind Your P's and E's," Worth 1996), not from price action.
- **Tenbagger math**: you only need a few big winners per lifetime; being right six times out of ten is excellent; size the wins, tolerate the errors (Frontline; "Charlie Silk's 150-Bagger," Worth 1994).
- **Six categories of stocks** — slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds, asset plays — his sorting vocabulary from *One Up on Wall Street*, applied constantly in the Worth columns (autos as cyclicals, banks, REITs, biotech).
- **Stomach over brain**: declines are scheduled tuition — "Far more money has been lost by investors preparing for corrections... than has been lost in corrections themselves" ("Fear of Crashing," Worth 1995); "the most important organ is the stomach" (Fidelity Viewpoints 2019).
- **Anti-macro, anti-timing**: "If you spend 13 minutes a year on economics, you've wasted 10 minutes" — no forecasting, only company-level inspection; mocks "dangerous sayings" ("it can't go lower," "it's already gone up so much") in the 1997 lecture; coined **diworseification** for empire-building acquirers.
- **Late-career consistency**: still preaching active, story-driven picking against the passive tide — "The move to passive is a mistake" (Bloomberg 2021); "'The sucker's going up' is not a good reason to buy" (CNBC 2023).

## Books
- **One Up on Wall Street** (1989; Millennium ed. 2000, with new introduction) — the doctrine: amateur's edge, six categories, tenbaggers, two-minute drill, diworseification — buy new (Simon & Schuster) or [borrow the 1989 edition at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/oneuponwallstree0000lync) (controlled digital lending).
- **Beating the Street** (1993; rev. 1994) — Magellan war stories plus his 21 "Peter's principles" and 1992 Barron's Roundtable picks with full reasoning — buy new or [borrow at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/beatingstreet00lync).
- **Learn to Earn: A Beginner's Guide to the Basics of Investing and Business** (1995) — capitalism and stock basics for beginners; most voice-diluted of the three — buy new or [borrow at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/learntoearnbegin00lync).

## Ingestion notes
- Highest voice-per-token: the ~46 Worth columns (pure first-person essays, ~1,500–2,500 words each, ~90k words total), the 1994 Press Club transcript (MOI version), the Frontline interview transcript, and the 2019 Fidelity Q&A — ingest these before any video.
- Rough corpus size: 3 books (~250k words) + Worth columns (~90k) + 4 long transcripts (~35k) + ~10 video items (~4 hours needing ASR) — call it ~400k words of authentic Lynch.
- Heavy dedup risk on the 1994 Press Club talk: it exists as C-SPAN video/CC, two-plus YouTube uploads, MOI Global transcript, brewbooks transcript, and countless quote posts — one underlying work. Same for the 1997 lecture (Investor Archive full, Yahoo Finance edit, "Ten Most Dangerous Things" excerpts) and the Dec 2021 Bloomberg interview (article + many clip channels).
- Transcript quality: C-SPAN's is uncorrected closed captioning; MOI Global's is edited for clarity ("may contain errors"); brewbooks omits the Q&A. Prefer MOI text cross-checked against video for quotes.
- Wayback worth.com pages render full column text but with 1990s nav clutter and occasional mid-word truncation on index pages; strip boilerplate ("Worth Online HOME . SEARCH...") before ingestion, and cross-check odd truncations against the Bishop Rock PDFs.
- YouTube entries were verified for existence/title/channel via YouTube's oEmbed API but not content-watched ([unverified-fetch]); clip-channel titles routinely mislabel dates ("rare new interview" is often decades-old footage), so date from internal evidence, not upload titles.
