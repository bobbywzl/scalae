# Sir John Templeton — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 02 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Founder of the Templeton Growth Fund (1954) and pioneer of global value investing; his rules, client letters, and interviews are canonical texts of bargain-hunting contrarianism.

## Voice profile — how Templeton thinks
- **Maximum pessimism**: buy "at the point of maximum pessimism," sell at maximum optimism — his self-declared signature, stated verbatim in the 1985 Adam Smith interview and the 16 Rules ("Bull markets are born on pessimism... die on euphoria").
- **Bargain hunting without borders**: the unit of analysis is the individual bargain stock, searched worldwide because "if you search worldwide, you will find more bargains and better bargains" (Rule 5; 1979 Empire Club address describes screening with dozens of "yardsticks of value").
- **One objective**: "maximum total real return after taxes" — after inflation and taxes, nothing else counts (Rule 1; Maxim 1).
- **Anti-crowd by arithmetic, not temperament**: "It is impossible to produce a superior performance unless you do something different from the majority" (maxims); buying what others are dumping is presented as logic, not bravado.
- **"This time it's different"** — flagged as "among the four most costly words in the annals of investing" (Rule 11); he reasons from long market history (his 1945 client letter cites the declines of 1921, 1932, 1938, 1942).
- **Flexibility and self-correction**: no permanently best asset class or method; stay open-minded, learn from mistakes, never invest with borrowed money, hold cash reserves at extremes (Rules 3, 12; 1987 Wall Street Week clips; 2005 "Financial Chaos" memo).
- **Planned, thrifty accumulation**: the client letters hammer "planned investment" over haphazard stock-picking; personally saved 50% of income (The Templeton Plan).
- **Optimistic spiritual empiricism**: relentless long-run optimism about human progress paired with epistemic humility ("How little we know, how eager to learn") — the worldview beneath the contrarianism, explicit in the 1988 Canadian Club speech and his spiritual books.
- **Reasoning style**: numbered rules, plain declarative sentences, teacherly letters to clients, cheerful delivery of unpopular conclusions, quantitative comparison shopping across countries and yardsticks.

## Tier 1 — Canon
- **[16 Rules for Investment Success](https://www.franklintempleton.com/forms-literature/download/TL-R16)** — 1993 · PDF (essay) · Franklin Templeton (official reprint) — His single most complete written statement of method, first published in *World Monitor*, Feb. 1993; byline "By Sir John Templeton." (Full text also at [ritholtz.com](https://ritholtz.com/2012/09/16-rules-for-investment-success/), verified.)
- **[The Templeton Letters (series hub)](https://web.archive.org/web/20191207125622/https://sirjohntempleton.org/category/templeton-letters/)** — 1940s–50s letters, posted 2010 · Blog posts reproducing client letters · Wayback capture of the defunct memorial site sirjohntempleton.org — ~10 posts quoting his actual investment-counsel letters ("Keeping a Client Happy" I–IV, "The Upside of a Down Market," "Investment Success," etc.). The live domain is now hijacked by spam — use Wayback only.
- **[The Templeton Letters: The First-Time Investor (1945 letter)](https://web.archive.org/web/20190922194957/https://sirjohntempleton.org/2010/04/15/the-templeton-letters-the-average-investor/)** — 1945 · Client letter text · Wayback — Long verbatim passage on planned vs. haphazard investing; the earliest Templeton voice freely online.
- **[John Templeton's Letter to Clients, circa 1954](https://www.templetonandphillips.com/s/TempletonLetter1954.PDF)** — c.1954 · Scanned letter PDF (3 pp., image-only, needs OCR) · Templeton & Phillips Capital Management ("From the Archives") — Original client letter from the year he launched the Templeton Growth Fund.
- **["The Idealism of Free Competition" — Letter to Clients, circa 1961](https://www.templetonandphillips.com/s/Freedom-John-Templeton-Letter.pdf)** — c.1961 · Scanned letter PDF (3 pp., image-only) · Templeton & Phillips — His argument for free competition as a moral system; investing worldview in his own hand.

## Books
- **The Humble Approach: Scientists Discover God** (1981) — his core spiritual-epistemology statement (humility theology) — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/humbleapproachsc0000temp).
- **The Templeton Touch** by William Proctor (1983; expanded ed. with Scott Phillips, 2012) — biography built on interviews with him; source of the 22 maxims — [1983 ed. on archive.org](https://archive.org/details/templetontouch0000proc); 2012 ed. via [Templeton Press/Rutgers UP](https://www.rutgersuniversitypress.org/templeton-press/the-templeton-touch/9781599473970/) [purchase].
- **The Templeton Plan: 21 Steps to Personal Success and Real Happiness** (with James Ellison, 1987) — his success/thrift principles — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/templetonplan21s0000temp).
- **Riches for the Mind and Spirit** (1990; reissued 2006) — his anthology of favorite wisdom passages with his commentary — purchase (Templeton Press); sampled free in the memorial booklet above.
- **Discovering the Laws of Life** (1994) — 200 short essays on his "laws of life" — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/discoveringlawso00temp).
- **Worldwide Laws of Life: 200 Eternal Spiritual Principles** (1997) — expanded laws-of-life collection; subject of the 1997 Charlie Rose interview — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/worldwidelawsofl0000temp).
- **Golden Nuggets from Sir John Templeton** (1997) — aphorism collection in his own words — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/goldennuggets0000temp).
- **Is Progress Speeding Up? Our Multiplying Multitudes of Blessings** (1997) — his data-driven optimism thesis — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/isprogressspeedi0000temp).
- **Investing the Templeton Way** by Lauren C. Templeton & Scott Phillips (2008) — niece's method book carrying his stories, sayings, and the 16 Rules — [archive.org controlled lending](https://archive.org/details/investingtemplet0000temp).
- **Templeton's Way with Money** by Alasdair Nairn & Jonathan Davis (2012) — built directly on his private fund memos and archive documents; the richest published source of his working memos — [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781119203001) [paywall] or purchase.

## Ingestion notes
- Highest voice-per-token, ingest first: 16 Rules PDF (~2,500 words), 22 maxims (~350 words), Financial Chaos memo (~700 words), 1979 Empire Club transcript (~4,500 words), the ~10 Templeton Letters posts (~300–600 words of letter text each), the two Templeton & Phillips letter scans (OCR required — image-only PDFs).
- Rough public corpus: ~15–20k words of clean primary text + 4–6 hours of interview video + 6 borrowable books; genuinely public *investing* prose is modest — the deep memo archive lives inside *Templeton's Way with Money* [paywall].
- Dedup warnings: the 16 Rules text is mirrored on dozens of blogs (keep only the Franklin Templeton PDF); the 22 maxims partially restate the 16 Rules; the three Templeton–Lynch clips appear to be recuts of one event; the 1990 interview video is duplicated across GuruFocus and YouTube; Charlie Rose exists on charlierose.com and YouTube — prefer the official transcript.
- Transcript quality: charlierose.com transcript is official; GuruFocus 1985 transcript is fan-made (spot-check against video); YouTube auto-captions on 1980s broadcast audio are poor — plan manual re-transcription for the Rukeyser-era clips.
- Domain hazard: sirjohntempleton.org is dead and currently serves gambling spam (verified Aug 2026) — never crawl the live domain; all its content (Templeton Letters, memorial booklet) must come from web.archive.org captures listed above.
- Dates in YouTube titles ("1985", "1987", "1990") are uploader-supplied; treat as approximate metadata until matched to episode guides.
