# Seth Klarman — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 05 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Seth Klarman (b. 1957) has run Boston's Baupost Group since 1982 and wrote the out-of-print cult classic *Margin of Safety*; he is the living investor most identified with disciplined, risk-first Graham-and-Dodd value investing, which is why his small public corpus punches far above its weight.

## Voice profile — how Klarman thinks
- **Loss avoidance before return seeking.** The core commandment across every document: "avoid losses" and the rest follows; risk is "how much can you lose and what are the chances of losing it," not volatility (MIT 2007 remarks; FAJ 2010 Q&A).
- **Margin of safety as the operating concept** — buying a dollar for 50–60 cents so that error, bad luck, and analytical imprecision are survivable; valuation is a range, "first and foremost an art, not a science."
- **Absolute, not relative performance.** He would rather lag a mania than lose capital; the 1999 Baupost Fund letter (on SEC EDGAR) defends underperforming the dot-com market "not because we abandoned our strict investment criteria but because we adhered to them."
- **Cash is a residual of the opportunity set.** "We are never fully invested if there is nothing great to do" (FAJ 2010); holding cash is the "painful decision" that trades risk of lost opportunity against risk of lost capital (2004 letter excerpt).
- **Edge comes from structural inefficiency**: illiquidity, complexity, urgency and forced selling, orphaned securities — "look for the most inefficient pockets in the world" (CNBC 2023); hence Baupost's multi-asset drift into distressed debt, real estate, and privates (Masters in Business 2026).
- **Markets as psychology plus analysis.** Value investing is "the marriage of a contrarian streak and a calculator"; Mr. Market is a counterparty to exploit, and crowd euphoria/panic is the raw material (Charlie Rose 2011; Forgotten Lessons of 2008).
- **Deep skepticism of leverage, financial engineering, and policy distortion** — the "everything bubble," Fed-suppressed rates, and the "false lessons" investors learn at tops (Forgotten Lessons of 2008; CNBC 2023).
- **Reasoning style**: bottom-up, case-by-case, aphoristic, historically literate, morally serious; long-view framing in which reputation, clients, and process compound together (Talks at GS 2025; HBS materials).

## Books
- **Margin of Safety: Risk-Averse Value Investing Strategies for the Thoughtful Investor** (1991, HarperBusiness) — his complete framework: risk aversion, value delusions, where bargains form (distress, spinoffs, complexity) — out of print; legitimate access is used copies (routinely $1,000+), library loan/ILL. All free PDFs are pirated.
- **Security Analysis, 6th ed.** (2008, McGraw-Hill; Graham & Dodd, Klarman lead editor) — contains his preface **"The Timeless Wisdom of Graham and Dodd"** (his best single essay on the discipline) plus his part-introductions — in print, Kindle, audio; no legitimate standalone hosting of the preface found (circulating PDFs are pirated).
- **Security Analysis, 7th ed.** (2023, McGraw-Hill; Klarman lead editor) — updated preface and essays including his modernizations ("sacrilege" edits) — in print/Kindle; his 2023 press round (CNBC, Capital Allocators, Institutional Investor) doubles as commentary on it.

## Ingestion notes
- **Highest voice-per-token, ingest first:** FAJ 2010 Q&A (11 pp.), MIT 2007 remarks (13 pp.), Forgotten Lessons of 2008, Painful Decision to Hold Cash, the EDGAR Baupost Fund N-30D letters (pull all N-30Ds under CIK 0000865827, 1996–2001 — some are financials-only, several contain full Klarman letters), and the 2026 Masters in Business transcript.
- **Rough corpus size:** ~10 core text documents ≈ 60–80k words, plus ~6–8 hours of audio/video (MiB ~90 min, Capital Allocators, VIWL 55 min, Talks at GS, Rose 46 min, two CNBC sittings) ≈ another ~60–70k words if transcribed. His non-public letters (40+ years) dwarf this; the public corpus is maybe 5% of his written output — be explicit about that ceiling.
- **Dedup warnings:** the Forgotten Lessons list and MIT speech are mirrored on dozens of blogs/scribd — ingest one canonical copy each; Bloomberg's 2026 clips ("Importance of Venture Capital," etc.) are cuts of the same MiB episode as the Ritholtz transcript; quote-compilation pages recycle lines from FAJ/MIT/letters, so attribute aphorisms to the underlying document, not the aggregator.
- **Transcript quality:** Ritholtz MiB transcript is machine-generated then lightly cleaned (names/numbers need checking); Capital Allocators transcript is paywalled — otherwise 2023–2026 audio needs your own ASR; Charlie Rose exists only as video + rough partial fan transcript; the 2006/2009 HBS clips are unofficial re-uploads that could vanish.
- **Voice drift:** pre-2011 Klarman is formal-didactic (speeches, letters); 2023–2026 Klarman is conversational and retrospective — tag documents by register and date so the model doesn't average them.
- **Legitimacy hygiene:** never ingest scribd/pdfcoffee copies of *Margin of Safety*, the Security Analysis prefaces, or full private-letter compilations; the EDGAR N-30D letters and the permissioned 2004 cash excerpt are the only full-letter texts that are cleanly public.
