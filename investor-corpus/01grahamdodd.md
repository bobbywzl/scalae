# Benjamin Graham & David Dodd — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 01 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Benjamin Graham (1894–1976) and David Dodd (1895–1988), Columbia Business School, invented security analysis as a discipline in *Security Analysis* (1934) and *The Intelligent Investor* (1949) — every value-investing text since is commentary on them.

## Voice profile — how Graham & Dodd think
- **Investment vs. speculation is the first distinction, always defined before anything else**: "An investment operation is one which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return. Operations not meeting these requirements are speculative" (*Security Analysis*, 1934).
- **Intrinsic value is real but approximate; price is opinion.** Asked by Senator Fulbright in 1955 how price rises to value, Graham answers: "That is one of the mysteries of our business... We know from experience that eventually the market catches up with value."
- **Margin of safety is the unifying concept** — buy so far below appraised value (net current assets, demonstrated earning power) that error, bad luck, and time are survivable; the 1932 Forbes series ("corporate gold dollars... at 50 cents") is the concept in action.
- **Mr. Market / the market as manic counterparty, not guide**: quotation is an offer to be exploited or ignored (*Intelligent Investor*, ch. 8); in 1976 he calls the exchanges "a John Bunyan type of Vanity Fair... that frequently degenerates into a madhouse."
- **Quantitative, rule-based, group-operation reasoning over story and forecast** — screens like "price below two-thirds of net quick assets" or the 7x-earnings/50%-equity rules of the 1976 Medical Economics piece; diversification does the work individual certainty cannot.
- **Reasoning style: worked arithmetic on real balance sheets, Socratic Q&A, classical allusion, and self-revision** — the 1946–47 lectures rebuild the 1940 edition example by example; late in life he openly downgrades "elaborate techniques of security analysis" in favor of a few simple criteria.
- **Two requirements for success**: "you have to think correctly; and secondly, you have to think independently" (to Hartman Butler, March 1976).
- **Dodd's role**: the meticulous co-architect and classroom systematizer; he published almost nothing solo (one 1930 thesis), so his voice lives in the co-authored editions of *Security Analysis*.

- Benjamin Graham Papers (correspondence, lecture notes, drafts, 1930s–1976) — not digitized; [Columbia RBML finding aid](https://findingaids.library.columbia.edu/ead/nnc-rb/ldpd_17325026) [unverified-fetch] for on-site research.

## Ingestion notes
- Highest voice-per-token: the ten 1946–47 lectures (spoken register, live Q&A), the 1963 San Francisco speech (Zweig's 2017 transcript is clean text with annotations), "A Conversation with Benjamin Graham" (1976), the Butler interview, and the 1955 testimony — ingest these before the books.
- Rough size: articles + speeches + interviews + testimony ≈ 250–350k words; the five Graham books add roughly 1M+ words; Graham-Newman letters ≈ 40–60 short pages of scans.
- Dedup hard: the 1946–47 lectures exist as (a) ten Columbia PDFs, (b) the compiled "Rediscovered Lectures" PDF, (c) the Wiley/print anthology; the 1963 speech exists as Zweig transcript, Zweig scan, valuehunter scan, and ValueWalk repost; the 1955 testimony exists as full GPO volume, Graham-only scan, and multiple blog excerpts; "The Decade 1965–1974" = "The Renaissance of Value" address (one document, two names).
- OCR caveat: most valuehunter/wordpress and Columbia Graham-Newman PDFs are image scans with no text layer (confirmed by fetch) — budget an OCR pass; the Forbes trio, *Future of Common Stocks*, and Medical Economics PDFs already have clean text.
- Edition drift matters: *Security Analysis* 1934/1940/1951/1962 differ substantially (Graham's voice fades in post-1962 editions edited by Cottle et al.); prefer 1934 + 1940 for voice modeling, and prefer 1949/1959-era *Intelligent Investor* text over the Zweig-commentary edition (keep Graham's text separate from Zweig's commentary when ingesting).
- Corrections from verification: the 1955 Fulbright transcript is not on FRASER (use the Google Books free ebook); "Toward a Science of Security Analysis" is 1952 (host mislabels 1945); the Medical Economics piece ran Sept 20, 1976; the Columbia "Superinvestors" PDF (`Buffett1984.pdf`) is dead — use the Columbia insights HTML page.
