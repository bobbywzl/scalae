# Joel Greenblatt — Primary-Source Corpus

> Scalae corpus library · file 06 of 09 · compiled 2026-08-12 · every link verified at compile time unless tagged [unverified-fetch] / [paywall] · see `00-INDEX.md` for tier semantics and ingestion order.

Founder of Gotham Capital (≈50%/yr gross, 1985–1994), co-founder of Value Investors Club and Gotham Asset Management, adjunct professor at Columbia Business School for two decades, and author of five books — canon because he turned Graham's value framework into a teachable system: special situations, then the "magic formula."

## Voice profile — how Greenblatt thinks
- One-sentence credo, repeated verbatim in his Columbia classes, Graham & Doddsville, and WealthTrack: **"Figure out what it's worth and pay a lot less."** Valuation first; everything else is discipline.
- **Special situations as structural edge**: spinoffs, restructurings, merger securities, rights offerings, recapitalizations — "look where nobody else is looking," because forced/uneconomic selling creates mispricing (*You Can Be a Stock Market Genius*, class notes 2002–2006).
- **Magic formula = cheap + good**: rank stocks on earnings yield (EBIT/EV) and return on tangible capital (EBIT / net working capital + net fixed assets); buy the best combined ranks. Buffett's "good business at a fair price" made quantitative (*The Little Book*, Forbes 2010 briefing, Talks at Google 2017).
- **Mr. Market pedagogy**: he opens his course with the gum-selling parable (Jason's gum business) and daily-quote thought experiments — prices swing far more than values; the market is a partner to trade against, not a guide (class notes, Class 1, Sept 7, 2005).
- **Value works because it doesn't always work**: "the secret to value investing is patience, and that's generally in short supply" — time arbitrage against institutional short-termism is the durable edge (Morningstar 2013, G&D Fall 2012).
- **Investing is simple but not easy**: the analysis is 8th-grade math (his stated goal in the *Little Book*); the failure mode is emotional — investors chase performance and "buy high, sell low," which is why he redesigned Gotham around strategies clients can stick with (WealthTrack 2014–2020, MiB 2018).
- **Career arc as argument**: 6–8 position concentrated book (returned outside capital in 1995) → diversified long/short "relative value" across hundreds of names — same valuation engine, different risk packaging (WealthTrack #1121, MiB 2020).
- **Beyond markets**: applies expected-value thinking to policy — alternative credentialing, Success Academy charter schools, immigration, bank capital — in *Common Sense* (2020) and the 2020–21 interview cycle (MiB 2020, TWiST E1159).
